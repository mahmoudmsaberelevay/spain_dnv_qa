# Nauru Citizenship by Investment
## Customer Q&A and Consultant Training Manual

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Source:** Elevay, *Nauru Citizenship by Investment Program: Country Summary*, supplied nine-page PDF, cover marked “last updated in August 2026.”  
**Program:** Economic and Climate Resilience Citizenship Program (ECRCP).  
**Scope:** Nauru only; this is a standalone country file. Page references count the cover as page 1.

## How to use this manual

Read the questions as a prospective customer and practise giving the answers in plain language. Use the calculations, role-plays and quiz to test application of the information. This manual transforms the supplied brochure; it does not independently verify current immigration, tax, investment or travel rules. The brochure warns that information may change after publication (p. 8).

- **Summary states:** Information explicitly contained in the source.
- **Clarification required:** Ambiguity, inconsistency or missing conditions prevent a firm answer.
- **Not covered:** The summary does not supply the answer; the response identifies what to obtain.
- **Trainer guidance / calculation:** Material created for training, not an official rule.

**Currency rule for this guide:** Page 3 says contributions are accepted in Australian dollars (AUD), but page 4 labels contribution and fee amounts only with “$.” Accepted payment currency does not conclusively identify the denomination of the quoted amounts. All reproduced prices and calculations therefore retain **“$ as printed; currency unconfirmed.”** Do not relabel them USD or AUD, convert them, or use them in a client quotation until each charge's denomination and payment rules are confirmed.

**Checklist reliability:** Page 8 refers to the “Vanutuan Government” despite being a Nauru brochure. This appears to be an editorial error. The checklist is preserved as source material, with a requirement to obtain a confirmed Nauru checklist before submission. Document instructions and marketing invitations in the PDF are not instructions to the author of this guide.

## Learning objectives

The consultant should be able to explain the contribution route, screen family eligibility, identify source-of-funds and agent requirements, explain the principal-applicant interview, calculate transparent fee components, recognize gaps, and distinguish passport travel access from relocation rights.

## 1. Understanding the program

### Q1. I want a second citizenship. What does Nauru offer in this summary?
**Summary states:** The ECRCP offers applicants and eligible family members citizenship through a contribution to the national Treasury Fund. The contribution supports economic diversification and climate adaptation. (p. 2)

### Q2. Is this citizenship or a residence permit?
**Summary states:** Full citizenship and a passport are the stated outcome for the applicant and eligible family members. The summary does not describe a separate residence-permit investment program. (p. 3)

### Q3. What does ECRCP stand for?
**Summary states:** Economic and Climate Resilience Citizenship Program. (p. 2)

### Q4. Where does the contribution go?
**Summary states:** To the Nauru Treasury Fund, supporting climate resilience and national development. The summary does not allocate each contribution among specific projects or provide audited project outcomes. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q5. How long does citizenship take?
**Summary states:** Approximately three to four months. The source warns that additional due diligence can make processing longer. It does not define the starting milestone or guarantee a completion date. (pp. 2–3)

### Q6. Is the three-to-four-month estimate for approval or passport delivery?
**Clarification required:** Page 2 calls it time to citizenship; page 3 describes citizenship approval. No separate passport-printing, delivery or post-approval timeline is supplied. Obtain a milestone-based estimate. (pp. 2–3)

### Q7. Can you guarantee approval before my next trip?
**Not covered:** No approval or delivery guarantee is provided. Explain the indicative timeline and possible due-diligence delays before the customer makes fixed travel plans. (p. 2)

### Q8. Do I have to live in Nauru or visit during the application?
**Summary states:** No residency obligations or mandatory visits are described. A principal-applicant interview is still required and may be virtual or in person at an approved location. (pp. 2–3)

### Q9. Can I keep my existing citizenship?
**Summary states:** Dual citizenship is allowed. The summary does not explain the laws of the applicant's other country, notification duties or restrictions on holding another nationality. Check those separately. (p. 2)

### Q10. Must I speak Nauruan or English, or pass an exam?
**Not covered:** Language, examination and education-qualification requirements are not stated. Do not interpret silence as an express waiver. The checklist's request for an education certificate is a document requirement, not a defined qualifying degree. (p. 8)

## 2. Bringing my family

### Q11. Can my spouse apply with me?
**Summary states:** A spouse is an eligible dependent category in the summary. Unmarried partners, separated spouses and multiple-spouse situations are not explained. (pp. 2–3)

### Q12. Can I include my children?
**Summary states:** Dependent children up to 30 years old, if unmarried, may be included. The source does not define the evidence of dependency or the date at which age and marital status are assessed. (pp. 2–3)

### Q13. My child is exactly 30. Can they apply?
**Summary states:** The phrase is “up to 30 years old if unmarried.” Confirm the exact cutoff and age-assessment date before promising eligibility for a person at the boundary. (pp. 2–3)

### Q14. Does an adult child have to attend university?
**Not covered:** The family section does not impose an express student condition. The checklist asks for an enrollment-letter copy for applicants who are currently at university. That document entry alone does not establish a universal university-enrollment eligibility requirement. (pp. 2–3, 8)

### Q15. Can a married child under 30 join?
**Clarification required:** The stated child category specifies unmarried children. The summary gives no married-child exception. Obtain an individual assessment rather than promising inclusion. (pp. 2–3)

### Q16. What if my child turns 31 or marries while processing?
**Not covered:** Age protection and changes in family circumstances are not explained. Resolve the applicable assessment date and reporting requirements before an age-sensitive application.

### Q17. Can my parents join?
**Summary states:** Dependent parents of the main applicant or spouse are mentioned. Minimum age, support thresholds and required evidence are not supplied. Do not import another country's parent age limit. (p. 2)

### Q18. Can I include grandparents?
**Not covered:** Grandparents are not expressly included in the family categories in this summary. Obtain confirmation rather than assuming “parents” includes them. (pp. 2–3)

### Q19. Can my brother or sister join?
**Summary states:** Siblings are included in the family description, and the contribution schedule lists $15,000 for each sibling of the principal applicant or their spouse. Age, marital status, dependency and consent requirements for siblings are not defined. (pp. 2–4)

### Q20. Can my spouse's sibling join?
**Summary states:** The sibling charge expressly refers to siblings of the principal applicant or their spouse. Specific eligibility conditions and proof of relationship still need confirmation. (p. 4)

### Q21. Does paying the sibling charge prove my sibling is eligible?
**Trainer guidance:** No. A fee line is not a complete eligibility rule. Confirm the person's relationship, age, marital status and any dependency conditions before quoting inclusion. (p. 4)

### Q22. Can I include stepchildren, adopted children or a child with a disability?
**Not covered:** Specific rules, exceptions, consent and supporting evidence for these situations are not supplied. Obtain a case-specific assessment without borrowing requirements from other programs.

### Q23. Is there a maximum number of dependents?
**Clarification required:** A contribution tier covers the applicant plus four or more dependents, but the summary does not state a maximum family size or all possible additional charges. That tier is not an unlimited eligibility guarantee. (p. 4)

### Q24. Can I add family members after citizenship is granted?
**Not covered:** Later additions, fees, application deadlines and changes in family status are not explained. The brochure's future-generation claim is not a complete later-addition procedure. (p. 3)

### Q25. Can my children pass citizenship to their children?
**Summary states:** Citizenship is described as transferable to future generations. The summary does not define descent conditions, generations, registration, fees or documentary procedures. Do not promise automatic passports for every descendant. (p. 3)

## 3. Eligibility and due diligence

### Q26. What is the minimum age for the principal applicant?
**Clarification required:** The general requirements say “at least 18,” while the detailed requirements on the same page say “over 18.” Obtain confirmation for an applicant exactly 18 and the date age is assessed. (p. 4)

### Q27. Must everyone have a clean criminal record?
**Summary states:** A clean background with no criminal record is required for the main applicant and dependents. The checklist separately requests police certificates for applicants aged 18 and above. These are different statements; the document threshold does not cancel the broader background requirement. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q28. Must I prove where my money came from?
**Summary states:** Yes. Applicants must demonstrate a lawful source of funds used for both the contribution and fees. Accepted evidence and treatment of gifts, loans, asset sales or business proceeds are not fully defined. (p. 4)

### Q29. What do AML and KYC mean in this process?
**Trainer explanation:** AML means anti-money-laundering checks; KYC means know-your-customer checks. The summary requires compliance with these standards and lawful source-of-funds evidence. Do not describe the program as a way to avoid identity or financial scrutiny. (p. 4)

### Q30. Can someone else sponsor or fund my application?
**Not covered:** Third-party sponsorship, permitted relationships and source-of-funds requirements for sponsors are not described. Obtain written acceptance and documentary requirements before arranging funding.

### Q31. Are any nationalities or jurisdictions restricted?
**Summary states:** The brochure refers to restricted jurisdictions, such as sanctioned countries, but supplies no complete list. It describes a condition involving lawful residence outside the country of origin for more than five consecutive years. This is not enough to decide an individual's eligibility. (p. 4)

### Q32. I have lived abroad for exactly five years. Does that satisfy the stated exception?
**Clarification required:** The wording says **more than five consecutive years**, not five years or more. The permitted countries, qualifying residence status and measurement date are not supplied. Obtain a written assessment. (p. 4)

### Q33. What proof of residence abroad does the summary mention?
**Summary states:** A residence permit is given as an example. The brochure does not define the complete proof package, permitted absences or continuity test. (p. 4)

### Q34. Does six years abroad automatically make a restricted applicant eligible?
**Clarification required:** No automatic approval follows from the summary. It says such applicants may only apply if the residence condition is met, but does not establish that this is the only condition or a complete exception for every restricted jurisdiction. (p. 4)

### Q35. Can I submit the application directly without an agent?
**Summary states:** All applications must go through licensed and accredited agents. Obtain the current agent authorization and engagement details for the intended submission arrangement. The brochure does not independently prove a particular person's accreditation. (p. 4)

### Q36. What happens if I previously had a visa refusal or legal issue?
**Not covered:** The consequences of visa refusals, investigations, litigation or other adverse history are not explained. Obtain the complete facts for a qualified case review before giving an eligibility assurance.

### Q37. Is there a minimum income or net-worth requirement beyond the contribution?
**Not covered:** No separate threshold is stated. The source-of-funds requirement, asset report and banking evidence do not themselves define a minimum net worth. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q40. Must my spouse or children attend interviews too?

### Q41. Is the interview fee included in due diligence?
**Not covered:** No separate interview price or inclusion statement is given. Obtain the charging arrangement rather than assuming the interview is free or included in the $10,000. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q42. What questions will I be asked, and can I use an interpreter?
**Not covered:** Topics, language support, disability accommodations, duration, rescheduling and retake procedures are not supplied. Obtain the interview instructions from the accredited case team.

### Q43. Must I travel for an oath, biometrics or passport collection?
**Clarification required:** The summary says no mandatory visits, but does not describe oath, biometrics or collection arrangements. Obtain the complete procedure before promising that every post-approval step is remote. (pp. 2–3)

### Q44. What are the official steps from submission to passport delivery?
**Not covered:** A full process map, payment milestones, approval sequence, document deadlines and passport-delivery procedure are not provided. Request the current official workflow.

### Q45. Can I appeal a refusal or resubmit?
**Not covered:** Refusal review, appeals, resubmission, waiting periods and associated fees are not stated. Obtain the applicable policy and deadlines before advising a refused applicant.

## 5. Contributions and fees

### Q46. What contribution amounts are printed?
**Summary states:** The schedule lists the tiers below. Currency is not identified alongside the figures; retain the dollar symbol as printed until confirmed. (p. 4)

| Applicant group | Contribution printed in the brochure |
|---|---:|
| Single applicant | $105,000 |
| Principal applicant + up to 3 dependents | $110,000 |
| Principal applicant + 4 or more dependents | $115,000 |
| Each sibling of principal applicant or spouse | $15,000 listed separately |

The applicant-plus-dependents tiers are read as family tiers; the explicit single-applicant row governs the single example. Confirm how siblings affect both the tier count and the separately stated charge.

### Q47. Are these Australian or US dollars?
**Clarification required:** The country currency is AUD and the summary says contributions are accepted in AUD, but the fee page uses only “$.” It does not expressly identify every quoted amount's denomination or conversion rules. Request a currency-specific written schedule before budgeting or paying. (pp. 2–4)

### Q48. Is this a refundable investment?
**Summary states:** No. The Treasury Fund contribution is explicitly non-refundable. The brochure describes no equity, property ownership, interest or capital repayment attached to it. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q49. Is there a property, business or bank-deposit alternative?
**Not covered:** The only citizenship route described is the Treasury Fund donation. Do not add investment alternatives from another country's guide. (p. 4)

### Q50. What application fee is listed?
**Summary states:** $25,000 **per application**, in addition to the contribution. This is one fee in the schedule, not the total price of the program. (p. 4)

### Q51. What due-diligence fees are listed?
**Summary states:** $10,000 for the main applicant and $7,500 per dependent **above 16 years old**. Confirm the exact age-16 boundary, whether any fee applies below that threshold, and the age-assessment date. (p. 4)

### Q52. Does the spouse pay the dependent due-diligence fee?
**Trainer interpretation requiring confirmation:** The spouse is named as a dependent category; an adult spouse above 16 is therefore treated as one $7,500 dependent in the exercises. The fee table does not supply a separate spouse row. Confirm this treatment in a live quotation. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q53. What is the passport fee?
**Summary states:** $500 per passport issued. The summary does not specify passport validity, renewal fees, replacement fees or whether delivery is additional. (p. 4)

### Q54. What bank charges are listed?
**Summary states:** Approximately $1,200 for a single applicant, with higher charges for families depending on the number of dependents. No family formula or fixed family amounts are provided. (p. 4)

### Q55. Should I budget two separate $15,000 sibling charges?
**Clarification required:** The same sibling figure appears in the contribution schedule and again as an example under additional dependents. The brochure does not say these are two cumulative charges. Do not double-count automatically; obtain one itemized statement explaining the sibling surcharge and contribution tier. (p. 4)

### Q56. Does $115,000 mean all extra dependents are free?
**Clarification required:** No. It is a contribution tier, while passport, applicable dependent due diligence, higher family banking costs and category-specific charges are additional. The source also warns that fees depend on family size and complexity. (p. 4)

### Q57. How much are professional fees?
**Summary states:** Provided upon request. Obtain the amount, currency, service scope, payment schedule, exclusions and refund policy. (p. 4)

### Q58. Can these fees change after I receive an estimate?
**Summary states:** The brochure says government and Elevay application fees are subject to change and depend on applicant numbers and process complexity. A quote should state its validity and treatment of later changes. (p. 4)

### Q59. What other expenses are missing from the printed schedule?
**Trainer guidance:** Ask about any applicable interview, medical, police, translation, certification, authentication, courier, transfer and passport-delivery expenses. These are items to check, not a confirmed list of additional mandatory charges. Do not invent amounts.

### Q60. When do I pay, and what is refunded after refusal or withdrawal?
**Not covered:** Payment milestones, escrow, authorized accounts and a full refusal/withdrawal policy are not supplied. The contribution is non-refundable, but that does not establish every fee's timing or refundability. Obtain written terms before transferring money. (p. 4)

## 6. Worked cost exercises

**Training arithmetic only.** Every amount below is **$ as printed; currency unconfirmed**. The exercises assume all components use the same currency only to practise addition. They are not valid currency-specific quotations until that assumption is checked.

Further assumptions:

- Each person qualifies; one application fee is charged.
- Each passport costs $500.
- Each dependent above 16, including an adult spouse, attracts $7,500 due diligence.
- No due-diligence charge is inserted for a child below 16 solely because none is printed; confirm this before a live quote.
- Professional, interview and other unquoted costs are excluded.
- Family bank charges remain unknown and are excluded rather than replaced with the single-applicant estimate.
- The sibling scenario adds the stated $15,000 once and assumes the sibling counts in the family tier; both points require confirmation.

| Example | Contribution | Application | Due diligence | Passports | Sibling addition | Known-component subtotal, excluding bank/professional/unlisted costs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A. Single adult | $105,000 | $25,000 | $10,000 | $500 | — | **$140,500** |
| B. Couple | $110,000 | $25,000 | $17,500 | $1,000 | — | **$153,500** |
| C. Couple + children aged 10 and 17 | $110,000 | $25,000 | $25,000 | $2,000 | — | **$162,000** |
| D. Same family as C + child aged 8 | $115,000 | $25,000 | $25,000 | $2,500 | — | **$167,500** |
| E. Same family as C + eligible unmarried dependent child aged 25 | $115,000 | $25,000 | $32,500 | $2,500 | — | **$175,000** |
| F. Principal + adult spouse + eligible adult sibling | $110,000 | $25,000 | $25,000 | $1,500 | $15,000 | **$176,500** |

### Q61. What does the single-applicant example become with the bank estimate?
**Calculation:** $140,500 + approximately $1,200 = **approximately $141,700**, still before professional and unlisted costs, and still with currency unconfirmed. The approximate bank line makes this an estimate, not an exact final price.

### Q62. How is the four-person example calculated?
**Calculation:** $110,000 contribution + $25,000 application + $10,000 main-applicant due diligence + $7,500 spouse due diligence + $7,500 for the 17-year-old + four passports at $500 = **$162,000**, before family banking and unquoted costs. The 10-year-old has no inserted due-diligence charge under the explicit exercise assumption.

### Q63. Why does adding an eight-year-old increase that subtotal by $5,500?
**Calculation:** The family moves from principal plus three dependents to principal plus four, raising the contribution by $5,000. One passport adds $500. Other possible changes, including higher family bank charges, are not quantified by the source.

### Q64. Why does an eligible 25-year-old add more than the eight-year-old?
**Calculation:** Moving from example C to E adds $5,000 contribution, $7,500 due diligence and $500 passport = **$13,000**, before other charges. Eligibility of the adult child must be confirmed independently of this calculation.

### Q65. Can I use these subtotals as a final family quotation?
**Clarification required:** No. Currency, family bank charges, professional fees, possible category-specific charges and any interview cost remain unresolved. Confirm the exact family, ages and eligibility, then obtain a complete itemized quote. The exercises demonstrate transparent arithmetic, not confirmed payable totals.

### Q67. Is the current passport's photo page sufficient?
**Summary states:** No. The entry requests all current-passport pages, plus a separate old-passport bio/current-visa-page copy. It does not explain how to handle an unavailable old passport or a visa in another document. (p. 8)

### Q68. Who needs a CV?
**Summary states:** The main applicant, spouse and dependents aged 18 and above. The required history period, format and treatment of gaps are not specified. (p. 8)

### Q69. How much banking history is requested?
**Summary states:** Twelve months of bank statements and an original bank reference letter for the main applicant. Minimum balances, accepted accounts and reference wording are not defined. (p. 8)

### Q70. What is the asset report?
**Summary states:** An original signed asset report is requested from the main applicant, and a draft will be provided. Required assets, valuation dates, supporting evidence and treatment of liabilities are not explained. Obtain the prescribed template. (p. 8)

### Q71. Do I need three professional reference letters?
**Clarification required:** The checklist says “three professional referees,” not three original letters. Confirm who may act as a referee, the relationship requirements, contact details and whether letters or direct checks are needed. Do not rewrite the entry as a confirmed letter requirement. (p. 8)

### Q72. Which police certificates are required?
**Summary states:** Original certificates from country of residence and country of origin for applicants aged 18+. The meaning of origin, treatment of multiple citizenships, prior residences and certificate validity are not specified. Do not import a ten-year residence-history rule from another program. (p. 8)

### Q73. My child is 17. Why might due diligence apply without a police certificate on the checklist?
**Summary states:** The fee schedule applies to dependents above 16; the police-certificate checklist begins at 18. These thresholds describe different things. Confirm whether additional documents will be requested for the 17-year-old rather than assuming the fee or background check disappears. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q74. Which medical tests are required?
**Summary states:** An original medical certificate no older than three months is requested for all applicants. The summary does not list specific tests, approved providers, exceptions or medical eligibility criteria. (p. 8)

### Q75. Are translations and apostilles required?
**Summary states:** The brochure says documents issued in Hague Convention member countries require apostilles. **Clarification required:** Translation language, accepted certifiers, non-member-country authentication and exceptions are not explained. Obtain Nauru-specific instructions, particularly given the checklist's wrong-country reference. (p. 8)

### Q76. How recent must my documents be?
**Summary states:** Proof of address and medical certificates must be no older than three months; the photo must have been taken within six months. No general validity period is supplied for other documents. Schedule collection around the confirmed submission process. (p. 8)

### Q77. Do I need a digital photo or printed photographs?
**Clarification required:** The checklist requests one JPEG photo per applicant, while the photo instructions mention high-quality paper and identical photos. Confirm whether a digital file alone is enough, any printed quantity, pixel dimensions and file-size limits. Do not guess them. (p. 8)

### Q78. What photo dimensions and appearance are listed?
**Summary states:** Approximately 35 × 45 mm; color; plain white background; sharp, clear and unaltered. A close front view of head and shoulders, with the head occupying 70–80%, eyes open and looking at the camera, no hair over the eyes, neutral expression and closed mouth. The image should be recent, within six months. (p. 8)

### Q79. Can I wear glasses or a head covering?
**Summary states:** No sunglasses; tinted prescription glasses may be worn if eyes remain visible. The instructions say nothing should cover part of the head but provide no religious or medical exceptions. Obtain the applicable Nauru photo standard for the applicant. (p. 8)

### Q80. Can the authorities request more documents?
**Clarification required:** The checklist has a generic additional-document warning, but it names the “Vanutuan Government.” Treat that as an apparent editorial error, not proof of Vanuatu jurisdiction or a confirmed Nauru procedure. Obtain the current Nauru checklist and additional-document policy; do not treat these 18 entries as an exhaustive guarantee. (p. 8)

## 8. Passport benefits, taxes and long-term questions

### Q81. How many countries can I visit?
**Clarification required:** The brochure claims 90 countries and territories, but the detailed list contains 92 entries when counted individually. It mixes unmarked destinations with visa-on-arrival, eVisa and ETA destinations. It cannot support a verified count of 90 strictly visa-free destinations. See Appendix A for the source list and legend. (pp. 2–3, 5)

### Q82. Does the brochure include the UK, Hong Kong, Singapore and UAE?
**Summary states:** Yes. The overview highlights them, and the detailed list includes them; the UK carries the source's ETA marker. These are brochure claims, not verified current entry rules. Confirm destination, nationality document, purpose, stay length and authorization before advising travel. (pp. 2, 5)

### Q83. What about South Korea and Russia?
**Summary states:** Both are named in the benefits section and the detailed destination list. No stay periods, entry purposes or restrictions are supplied. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q84. Does a visa-free or ETA destination let me work or settle there?
**Not covered:** The travel list supplies no foreign employment, study or residence rights. Ask what the client intends to do in the destination and verify the relevant rules separately.

### Q85. Does using Australian dollars give me Australian or New Zealand migration rights?
**Not covered:** The brochure supplies no such right. A country's currency does not establish another country's immigration entitlement. Neither Australia nor New Zealand appears in its travel list. (pp. 2, 5–6)

### Q86. Is the US covered because Guam is on the list?
**Clarification required:** Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands are listed separately. The brochure provides no basis to extend those entries to all US territory or mainland entry, and no qualifying conditions are given. Verify the exact destination instead of generalizing. (p. 5)

### Q87. Will I stop paying capital gains, inheritance or worldwide income taxes?
**Clarification required:** Page 2 makes an unqualified tax claim; page 3 applies the claim to non-residents. The summary does not define tax residence, source of income, other tax liabilities or obligations in the client's existing countries. It cannot determine personal tax treatment, especially if the client plans to move to Nauru. (pp. 2–3)

### Q88. Will my banking and investments become confidential?
**Clarification required:** The brochure mentions financial privacy but supplies no reporting, disclosure or beneficial-ownership rules. Do not describe citizenship as an exemption from lawful disclosure or source-of-funds obligations. (pp. 3–4)

### Q89. How long does the passport last, and what does renewal cost?
**Not covered:** Validity by age, renewal process, renewal fees, replacement costs and delivery details are not stated. The $500 issuance charge is not a confirmed renewal schedule. (p. 4)

### Q90. Can citizenship be revoked, and what obligations will we have?
**Not covered:** Revocation, loss of nationality, civic duties, military obligations, ongoing reporting and later physical-presence rules are not comprehensively explained. Obtain the applicable legal conditions before advising on long-term status.

## 9. Living in Nauru and adviser support

### Q91. Where is Nauru, and what is its administrative center?
**Summary states:** It is in Micronesia in the Central Pacific. The brochure labels Yaren the de facto administrative center, describes Nauru as the world's third-smallest country by land area and gives its area as 21 km². (p. 6)

### Q92. What language, currency and government are described?
**Summary states:** Nauruan is described as official and widely spoken, with English used in government and business. The currency is AUD, the time zone UTC+12, and the government a unitary parliamentary republic. These country facts do not settle the currency denomination of the fee table. (p. 6)

### Q93. What lifestyle and economy does the summary describe?
**Summary states:** It promotes a peaceful Pacific island lifestyle, warm climate, political stability, a neutral international stance, a stable legal system and access to Pacific trade networks. It recounts historic phosphate wealth and describes diversification into services, renewable energy and climate resilience. These statements do not establish a specific business entitlement, current safety assessment or guaranteed investment opportunity. (pp. 2–3, 6)

### Q94. What will housing, schools and healthcare cost my family?
**Not covered:** No household budget, rental inventory, school options, medical facilities, insurance costs or specialist-care arrangements are given. The general healthcare/education marketing claims do not establish free services or admission rights. Obtain a separate relocation brief. (p. 3)

### Q95. What about flights, jobs, internet, banking and climate risks?
**Not covered:** Flight frequency, work opportunities, salaries, internet quality, account opening, housing availability and climate-risk planning are not detailed. Climate resilience is a stated program purpose, not a complete assessment of the family's future living conditions.

### Q96. Who is Elevay, and what does it say it does?
**Summary states:** Elevay describes itself as a Dubai-based international citizenship and residency planning organization with over 25 years of experience and hundreds of satisfied clients. ## 10. Consultation workflow and discovery prompts

**Trainer-created workflow, not the official application sequence.**

1. **Define the objective:** Second citizenship, practical relocation, travel, education, family planning or business. Identify any specific destination and activity outside Nauru.
2. **Map the family:** Exact ages, relationships, marital status, dependency and likely future changes. Flag principal age 18, dependent age 16 and child age 30.
3. **Screen eligibility:** Citizenship/origin, residence history, restricted-jurisdiction issues, criminal or immigration history and lawful funds.
4. **Confirm the submission route:** Identify the licensed accredited agent and written service scope.
5. **Resolve currency before quoting:** Confirm denomination of every charge, payment currency, conversion date/rate and bank charges.
6. **Build the cost sheet:** Base contribution, one application fee, principal/dependent due diligence, passports, sibling treatment, family banking, professional and other applicable costs.
7. **Obtain the corrected checklist:** Resolve the Vanuatu reference, photo format, referee requirements, age wording and authentication.
8. **Arrange interview and milestones:** Confirm participants, format, location, charges, payment stages and realistic timing.
9. **Plan relocation separately:** Obtain practical living and individual tax information if the family will actually move.

### Suggested discovery questions

- What is the primary reason you want Nauru citizenship?
- Do you plan to live there, or is travel flexibility the main goal?
- Which countries and activities matter most to you?
- Who will apply, and what are their exact ages, relationships and marital status?
- How are adult children and parents financially supported?
- Are siblings involved, and are they siblings of the principal or spouse?
- What are each person's citizenships, origins and residence histories?
- If a restricted-jurisdiction issue exists, what proves continuous lawful residence abroad?
- Where did the contribution and fee funds originate?
- What currency is your budget in, and what timing or liquidity constraints apply?
- Are there school, healthcare, accessibility, employment or family deadlines to address?

## 12. Role-play exercises

### Scenario A: Single applicant thinks the headline is all-inclusive

**Customer:** “I have $105,000. That covers the passport, right?”

**Expected response:** Explain contribution versus fees and the currency ambiguity. Show the $140,500 known-component subtotal and approximately $141,700 with the printed single bank estimate, both before professional and other costs and with currency unconfirmed. Do not present either as a final quote.

### Scenario B: Family of five

**Customer:** “My spouse and children aged 8, 10 and 17 are joining. Is the contribution still $110,000?”

**Expected response:** Count four dependents, so the printed contribution tier is $115,000. Show the $167,500 known-component subtotal under the exercise assumptions. Explain that the 17-year-old attracts dependent due diligence and family bank fees remain unquoted.

### Scenario C: Adult sibling

**Customer:** “My spouse's brother will come too. Is the sibling charge $15,000 or $30,000?”

**Expected response:** Explain that the brochure repeats the same figure without establishing two cumulative fees. Confirm the sibling's eligibility, tier-count treatment and actual surcharge in writing. Do not infer eligibility solely from the fee row.

### Scenario D: Restricted-jurisdiction applicant

**Customer:** “I have legally lived abroad for exactly five years, so I qualify automatically.”

**Expected response:** The source says more than five consecutive years and provides no complete restriction list or exception criteria. Ask for the residence history and evidence, then obtain a case assessment without promising eligibility.

### Scenario E: Checklist inconsistency

**Customer:** “Should I prepare one JPEG or printed photos? Why is Vanuatu mentioned?”

**Expected response:** Identify both source issues openly. Obtain a corrected Nauru checklist and photo specification. Preserve the source requirements in training, but do not guess the submission format.

### Scenario F: Travel and tax promises

**Customer:** “I can live in the UK and stop paying taxes worldwide after approval, correct?”

**Expected response:** The UK travel entry carries an ETA marker, but the list does not establish UK residence or work rights. The tax statements are broad and partly qualified for non-residents. Ask about actual residence, activities and existing obligations, then seek current destination and individual tax advice.
