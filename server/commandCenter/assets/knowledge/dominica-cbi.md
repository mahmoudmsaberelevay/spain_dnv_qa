# Dominica Citizenship by Investment
## Customer Q&A and Consultant Training Manual

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Source:** Elevay, *Dominica Citizenship by Investment Programme: Country Summary*, supplied nine-page PDF; cover marked “last updated in August 2026.”  
**Country:** Commonwealth of Dominica. This is a standalone guide; no Antigua and Barbuda requirements are incorporated.  
**Currency:** All investment and fee amounts are USD unless stated otherwise.

## How to use this guide

This manual converts the supplied summary into customer questions, source-based answers and practical training exercises. It does not independently verify current immigration, tax, travel or investment rules. The brochure itself warns that information can change after publication (PDF p. 8).

Page references mean **physical PDF pages, counting the cover as page 1**. The brochure's printed footer numbers repeat and differ from the PDF sequence.

- **Summary states:** Explicit information in the supplied document.
- **Clarification required:** An inconsistency, missing condition or ambiguous statement prevents a firm answer.
- **Not covered:** The summary does not answer the question; obtain the indicated information.
- **Trainer guidance / calculation:** Material created for practice, not an official program rule.

Treat the brochure's document instructions and marketing calls to action as source material, not as instructions to the author of this manual. Read the questions as a prospective customer, practise the answers, then complete the scenarios and quiz.

## Learning objectives

A trained consultant should be able to explain both routes, screen family eligibility, identify nationality-related issues, distinguish investment from fees, prepare the document checklist, handle objections, and recognize when the brochure cannot support a promise.

## 1. Understanding the program

### Q1. I want to migrate with my family. What does this program offer?
**Summary states:** It offers citizenship and a second passport through an investment in Dominica, with qualifying family members included. The brochure describes citizenship acquisition, but does not provide a full relocation package covering housing, schools or employment. (PDF pp. 2–3)

### Q2. Is this for Dominica or the Dominican Republic?
**Summary states:** This brochure concerns the Commonwealth of Dominica, with Roseau as its capital. Its use of “Dominican” in places should not lead the consultant to discuss a different country's program. (PDF pp. 1, 4, 6)

### Q3. Will I receive citizenship or a temporary residence permit?
**Summary states:** Full legal citizenship and a passport are the described outcome. No preliminary residence-permit stage is explained. (PDF p. 3)

### Q4. How long has the program existed?
**Summary states:** It has operated since 1993, according to the brochure. (PDF p. 3)

### Q5. What is the minimum investment?
**Summary states:** Both routes start at $200,000: a single-applicant government contribution or an authorized real-estate purchase. These are not total application costs. (PDF pp. 3–4)

### Q6. How long will citizenship take?
**Clarification required:** The overview paragraph and delay footnote refer to 6–9 months, while the time-to-citizenship box and benefits page say 9–12 months. Additional due diligence may cause longer processing. Obtain a current estimate and its starting milestone rather than guaranteeing either range. (PDF pp. 2, 5)

### Q7. Can you guarantee completion before my child's school year starts?
**Not covered:** The brochure supplies no binding completion deadline or approval guarantee. Explain the conflicting estimates and plan around confirmed milestones rather than committing to a fixed date. (PDF p. 2)

### Q8. Do I have to live in Dominica before applying or after approval?
**Summary states:** The overview lists no residency requirement. It does not fully explain travel for interviews, an oath, passport collection or any later formalities. Do not infer that every step is necessarily remote. (PDF p. 2)

### Q9. Must I speak English or pass an exam?
**Summary states:** No language requirement is listed. The benefits section also says no education or management requirements. The document checklist nevertheless requests adult education certificates. (PDF pp. 2, 5, 8)

### Q10. Can I keep my existing passport?
**Summary states:** Dominica permits dual citizenship. The summary does not establish whether the applicant's existing country allows dual citizenship or imposes notification requirements. Check that separately. (PDF pp. 2, 5)

## 2. Bringing my family

### Q11. Can I include my spouse?
**Summary states:** A spouse of the main applicant is listed as a dependent category. Rules for unmarried partners, multiple spouses or separated spouses are not supplied. (PDF p. 3)

### Q12. Can I include children under 18?
**Summary states:** A child under 18 of the main applicant or their spouse is included in the detailed dependent categories. Custody and consent requirements are not explained. (PDF p. 3)

### Q13. Can my adult child qualify?
**Summary states:** The detailed list describes a child between 18 and 30 attending a recognized higher-learning institution and substantially supported by the main applicant or spouse. Age alone does not establish eligibility. (PDF p. 3)

### Q14. My child is exactly 30. Are they eligible?
**Clarification required:** The detailed list says “between eighteen and thirty,” while the benefits page says “under 30.” Confirm the exact cutoff and when age is measured. (PDF pp. 3, 5)

### Q15. My 24-year-old works and is not studying. Can they join?
**Clarification required:** The ordinary adult-child category specifies higher education and substantial support. The brochure does not establish eligibility for a financially independent, non-student adult child. Request an assessment rather than relying on the overview's general reference to dependent children. (PDF pp. 2–3)

### Q16. What evidence does my university student need?
**Summary states:** The checklist requests an original enrollment letter for university students and education certificates for adult applicants. It also requests a support affidavit for dependents over 18, excluding the spouse. The exact evidence of substantial support and accepted institutions is not defined. (PDF pp. 3, 8)

### Q17. Can an adult child with a disability qualify?
**Summary states:** A child aged 18 or older who is physically or mentally challenged, using the brochure's terminology, and substantially supported by the main applicant or spouse is listed. No upper age is stated for this category. Medical evidence and assessment criteria require clarification. (PDF p. 3)

### Q18. Can I include children from my spouse's previous relationship?
**Summary states:** Children of the applicant's spouse are expressly included in the relevant child categories. The summary does not explain custody, other-parent consent, adoption or stepchild evidence. (PDF p. 3)

### Q19. Can my parents or grandparents join?
**Summary states:** Parents or grandparents of the main applicant or spouse above 65, substantially supported by the applicant or spouse, are listed. (PDF p. 3)

### Q20. My mother is exactly 65. Can she qualify?
**Clarification required:** Eligibility wording says above 65, while the due-diligence table labels dependent parents “65+.” A fee category is not proof of eligibility. Obtain a written answer for the boundary age. (PDF pp. 3–4)

### Q21. Can I include my spouse's parents?
**Summary states:** Yes, the relationship is listed, subject to the stated age and substantial-support conditions. The evidence needed to demonstrate support is not specified. (PDF p. 3)

### Q22. The overview mentions unmarried daughters. Is that a separate category?
**Clarification required:** The overview names unmarried daughters, but the detailed dependent list does not set out a separate rule for them. Do not assume unmarried status waives the adult-child education, age or support requirements. (PDF pp. 2–3)

### Q23. Can my brother or sister be included?
**Not covered:** Siblings do not appear in this summary's dependent list. It provides no basis to promise inclusion. Do not import sibling rules from another country's program. (PDF p. 3)

### Q24. Can I add a spouse or child after receiving citizenship?
**Not covered:** Later additions, deadlines, categories and fees are not set out. The brochure's claim about citizenship by descent does not answer every post-approval family-addition question. (PDF p. 5)

### Q25. What happens if a child turns 18, graduates or marries during processing?
**Not covered:** The relevant assessment date, continued-dependency rules and changes in circumstances are not explained. Request instructions for the specific event before deciding eligibility or fees.

## 3. Eligibility, restrictions and interviews

### Q26. What are the main applicant's minimum age, income and net worth?
**Not covered:** No thresholds are supplied. Do not infer them from dependent ages or investment amounts. Obtain the current applicant criteria.

### Q27. Do I need a clean criminal record?
**Summary states:** Applicants must have a clean criminal record from their country of origin. The checklist additionally requires police certificates for applicants aged 16 or above from specified countries. (PDF pp. 5, 8)

### Q28. Are there nationality restrictions?
**Summary states:** Yes. The brochure lists bans, restrictions, possible exceptions, case-by-case reviews and enhanced due diligence. Its wording is not fully consistent and includes a March 2022 statement; it cannot alone establish current individual eligibility. The full reported categories appear below. (PDF p. 5)

| Group named in the brochure | Brochure statement | Training treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Iraq / Kurdistan region | Applications banned or not accepted; named cities include Erbil, Amedi, Dahuk, Kirkuk, Lalish, Raniya, Soran, Sulaymaniyah and Zakho | Confirm applicability to the individual's citizenship, origin and residence; do not reinterpret regional labels as distinct citizenships |
| North Korea and Sudan | Refusal unless applicant has not lived there for at least 10 years, has no substantial assets there, and has not conducted business or similar activity in or with those countries | Confirm the scope and current availability of all stated exceptions |
| Russia and Belarus | From March 2022, new applications refused; already submitted applications continue processing | Historical statement in the brochure, not a current case determination |
| Afghanistan, Chechnya, Pakistan, Sao Tome Principe, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan and Yemen | Case-by-case treatment; mentions exceptions for legal residence elsewhere over 10 years and funds not originating in a restricted country | Preserve the brochure's labels without assuming each is a nationality; obtain a case-specific assessment |
| Sudan and Syria | Separately described as restricted | Reconcile Sudan's repeated treatment and clarify any exception |
| Iran | References S.R.O. No. 8 of 2021 and enhanced due diligence with special fees | The sentence about “specified country” status is malformed; confirm eligibility and applicable treatment |

### Q29. I am from a restricted country but have lived elsewhere for twelve years. Am I automatically eligible?
**Clarification required:** No automatic conclusion follows. The summary describes different conditions for different groups, including assets, business ties, legal residence and origin of funds. Long residence abroad alone does not establish that all conditions are met. (PDF p. 5)

### Q30. What if I am Sudanese?
**Clarification required:** Sudan appears both in a conditional-exception provision and in a separate restriction statement. Obtain a written case assessment rather than choosing the more favorable sentence. (PDF p. 5)

### Q31. Can Iranian applicants apply at the normal cost?
**Summary states:** The brochure supplies a separate enhanced due-diligence schedule for Iranian applicants. Its wording about specified-country status is unclear, so the existence of a fee table must not be treated as guaranteed eligibility. (PDF pp. 4–5)

### Q32. Are interviews compulsory?
**Summary states:** Interviews are mandatory for all applicants aged 16 or older. A fee of $1,000 is listed for each non-Iranian applicant aged 16+. The Iranian interview-fee treatment is not supplied. (PDF p. 4)

### Q33. Must my 16-year-old child attend an interview?
**Summary states:** Yes, the stated interview threshold includes age 16. The format, location, language support and arrangements for minors are not explained. (PDF p. 4)

### Q34. Can the interview be online or waived?
**Not covered:** Remote attendance, exemptions, disability accommodations, interview topics and rescheduling are not described. Obtain the applicable instructions.

### Q35. What if I have had a visa refusal or legal problem?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not state the effect of prior visa refusals, proceedings or other adverse history. Obtain the full facts for case review before giving an eligibility assurance.

## 4. Choosing my investment

### Q36. What are the two investment options?
**Summary states:** A contribution to the Economic Diversification Fund (EDF) or authorized real estate with a minimum value of $200,000. The contribution amount varies by family, and the property route has separate government fees. (PDF pp. 3–4)

### Q37. What is the EDF option?
**Summary states:** A non-refundable payment to the Commonwealth of Dominica. It begins at $200,000 for one applicant, with a family contribution schedule. The summary does not provide a detailed allocation of the money to public projects. (PDF pp. 3–4)

### Q38. What donation applies to a family of four?
**Summary states:** $250,000 for the stated example of the main applicant, one spouse and two dependent children. Do not automatically apply this composition to every four-person family without checking. (PDF p. 4)

### Q39. What if only my spouse and I apply, or we have one child?
**Not covered:** The donation table states a single-applicant amount and a family-of-four amount, but does not explicitly price families of two or three. Obtain a written contribution quote. Do not fill the gap with another program's schedule. (PDF p. 4)

### Q40. What does an additional dependent cost under the EDF route?
**Summary states:** The contribution schedule lists $25,000 per additional dependent under 18 and $40,000 per additional dependent aged 18 or above. Additional due diligence, interviews and applicable program charges must be considered separately. Confirm the relevant base-family category. (PDF p. 4)

### Q41. Will the government return my donation later?
**Summary states:** The EDF payment is explicitly non-refundable. The brochure does not explain when it becomes payable or the full refusal/withdrawal treatment for all payments. (PDF p. 4)

### Q42. Can I buy any house in Dominica?
**Summary states:** The real estate must be authorized, with a minimum value of $200,000. The brochure gives no approved-project list or property-selection criteria. (PDF p. 3)

### Q43. What government fees apply to the property route?
**Summary states:** $75,000 for a single applicant; $100,000 for a family of up to four; $25,000 for each additional dependent under 18; and $40,000 for each additional dependent aged 18 or above. These are in addition to the property investment and other listed charges. (PDF p. 4)

### Q44. What is the 4% share registration fee?
**Summary states:** The real-estate schedule lists a 4% share registration fee. **Clarification required:** It does not define the calculation base, applicability to different ownership structures, or whether other purchase charges apply. Do not assume 4% of the whole application cost. (PDF p. 4)

### Q45. When can I sell the property?
**Summary states:** The summary says the property may not be sold for at least three years and that it may then be sold to another applicant under the same program. The overview also says “exit strategy: 3 years.” The starting date and detailed resale-eligibility rules are not supplied, so verify them before promising an exit. (PDF pp. 2, 4)

### Q46. Does three years mean I am guaranteed a buyer or my original money back?
**Not covered:** The brochure supplies no guaranteed resale, capital protection, rental yield or buyback. A stated holding period is not an assurance of liquidity or value. Obtain the investment contract and exit terms.

### Q47. Can I live in the property or rent it out?
**Not covered:** Personal-use rights, rental arrangements, maintenance, insurance, taxes and management fees are not described. Obtain the project's ownership and operating terms.

### Q48. Can I finance the investment, buy jointly or pay in installments?
**Not covered:** The summary does not specify financing, joint ownership or installment arrangements. Confirm eligibility and payment terms for the proposed structure.

### Q49. Is there a business or university investment route in this summary?
**Summary states:** Only EDF and real estate are described. The summary does not provide a separate business or university route. Do not copy options from the previous country's guide. (PDF p. 3)

### Q50. Which option is best for my family?
**Trainer guidance:** First establish eligibility, family composition, total budget, liquidity needs and willingness to own property. Compare the donation's non-refundable nature with the property's costs, terms and risks. The summary alone cannot establish the best route for an individual customer.

## 5. Understanding the full cost

### Q51. Is $200,000 the total amount I will pay?
**Summary states:** No. The schedules add program charges, due diligence, interviews and professional fees. Real estate also has government fees and a 4% share registration fee. (PDF p. 4)

### Q52. What standard due-diligence fees are listed?
**Summary states:** Both routes show the same schedule. The separate Iranian schedule is addressed in Q53. (PDF p. 4)

| Category | Listed standard fee |
|---|---:|
| Main applicant | $7,500 |
| Spouse | $4,000 |
| Dependent aged 16+ | $4,000 each |
| Dependent parent aged 65+ | $4,000 each |

No standard under-16 fee is expressly stated. The parent row should not be added a second time to the same person's dependent fee. Grandparents are not separately named in the fee table; confirm their treatment.

### Q53. What enhanced fees are listed for Iranian applicants?
**Summary states:** $25,000 for the main applicant, $15,000 for the spouse and each dependent aged 16+, and $10,000 for each dependent aged 12–15. The brochure does not expressly state a fee for children below 12 or how mixed-nationality families are assessed. Do not add standard and enhanced schedules together without an explicit instruction. (PDF pp. 4–5)

### Q54. What are the other program charges?
**Summary states:** Both routes list the charges below. Most charging units are not defined, so the final quote must confirm per-person versus per-application treatment. (PDF p. 4)

| Charge | Amount in brochure | Point requiring confirmation |
|---|---:|---|
| Passport | $60 | Per passport/person and any additional issuance costs |
| Optional expedited passport process | $1,200 | Charging unit, speed and eligibility |
| Processing | $1,000 | Per application or person |
| Naturalization, including stamp fees | $500 | Per application or person |
| Bank | $250 | Per application, payment or transfer |
| Interview | $1,000 for non-Iranian applicants aged 16+ | Iranian treatment and arrangements |
| Professional services | On request | Complete scope, amount and exclusions |

### Q55. Does the $1,200 expedited option make citizenship approval faster?
**Summary states:** It is described as an expedited **passport** process, not expedited citizenship approval. No delivery time is supplied. Do not sell it as a way to bypass due diligence or guarantee earlier approval. (PDF p. 4)

### Q56. Do Iranian applicants get a free interview because their fee is not listed?
**Clarification required:** No such exemption is stated. Interviews are described as mandatory for all applicants 16+, but the $1,000 line expressly refers to non-Iranians. Obtain the applicable fee rather than treating missing pricing as zero. (PDF p. 4)

### Q57. Are children below the due-diligence thresholds definitely free of all fees?
**Clarification required:** No. Absence of a due-diligence fee row does not eliminate other contribution, government, passport or program charges. Confirm any unlisted due-diligence treatment before quoting. (PDF p. 4)

### Q58. What do professional fees cover?
**Not covered:** The brochure says they are provided on request. Ask for the engagement scope, legal/submission services, document work, payment schedule, exclusions and refund terms. (PDF p. 4)

### Q59. What other costs should I ask about?
**Trainer guidance:** Obtain any applicable certification, apostille, translation, medical, courier, travel, transfer, property legal, maintenance and transaction costs. These amounts are not priced in the summary; do not call a sum of printed fees an all-inclusive quote.

### Q60. When do I pay, and what happens if I am refused or withdraw?
**Not covered:** The summary does not provide payment milestones, escrow arrangements, authorized payment instructions or a complete refund policy. The EDF is expressly non-refundable, but that does not explain every payment's timing or refundability. Obtain written route-specific terms. (PDF p. 4)

## 6. Worked budget exercises

**Trainer-created calculations, not current quotations.** The brochure omits several charging units. To make the arithmetic reviewable, these exercises explicitly assume:

- Applicants qualify and use the standard, non-Iranian due-diligence schedule.
- Passport fee: $60 per person; naturalization: $500 per person.
- Processing: $1,000 per application; bank fee: $250 per application.
- Interview: $1,000 per non-Iranian applicant aged 16+.
- No due-diligence fee is added for an under-16 child **solely because none is listed**; this must be confirmed.
- No optional expedited service, professional fees or unlisted costs are included.
- Property illustrations assume the 4% share fee applies to a $200,000 investment, producing $8,000. Its actual base and applicability require confirmation.

| Example | Contribution / property | Property government fee | Due diligence | Interviews | Assumed program charges* | Assumed share fee | Illustrative subtotal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A. Single, EDF | $200,000 | — | $7,500 | $1,000 | $1,810 | — | **$210,310** |
| B. Couple + children aged 10 and 17, EDF | $250,000 | — | $15,500 | $3,000 | $3,490 | — | **$271,990** |
| C. Same family + eligible child aged 8, EDF | $275,000 | — | $15,500 | $3,000 | $4,050 | — | **$297,550** |
| D. Same family as B + eligible university student aged 20, EDF | $290,000 | — | $19,500 | $4,000 | $4,050 | — | **$317,550** |
| E. Single, real estate | $200,000 | $75,000 | $7,500 | $1,000 | $1,810 | $8,000 | **$293,310** |
| F. Same family as B, real estate | $200,000 | $100,000 | $15,500 | $3,000 | $3,490 | $8,000 | **$329,990** |

*Program charges in these exercises = $1,000 processing + $250 bank + ($60 passport + $500 naturalization) × number of applicants. Interview fees are shown separately. Property investment remains an asset purchase, subject to its terms; fees are separate cash outlays. No resale proceeds are assumed.

### Q61. How was the family-of-four EDF example calculated?
**Calculation:** $250,000 contribution + $7,500 main-applicant due diligence + $4,000 spouse due diligence + $4,000 for the 17-year-old + $3,000 interviews + $240 passports + $2,000 naturalization + $1,000 processing + $250 bank = **$271,990**. The 10-year-old is assigned no due-diligence charge only under the exercise assumption.

### Q62. What would an extra child add to that family budget?
**Calculation:** An eligible eight-year-old adds $25,000 contribution and $560 assumed passport/naturalization charges, totaling **$25,560**. An eligible 20-year-old student adds $40,000 contribution, $4,000 due diligence, $1,000 interview and $560 assumed charges, totaling **$45,560**. These calculations exclude unlisted costs and require the same fee confirmations.

### Q63. How much higher is the property example for the same four people?
**Calculation:** $329,990 minus $271,990 = **$58,000** in additional illustrated initial cash outlay. The property example includes a $200,000 asset purchase; do not describe the comparison as equivalent permanent costs or guaranteed investment returns.

### Q64. How would enhanced due diligence change a family calculation?
**Calculation:** For an Iranian main applicant, spouse, child aged 17 and child aged 13, the brochure's enhanced due-diligence component alone is $25,000 + $15,000 + $15,000 + $10,000 = **$65,000**. Do not turn this into a complete quote without confirming eligibility, interview pricing and all other charges.

### Q65. Why not show a final quote for a couple using the donation route?
**Clarification required:** The brochure does not explicitly state a two-person donation amount. A responsible training answer is to identify the missing base amount and request it, rather than invent a precise total.

### Q67. Is the passport photo page enough?
**Summary states:** No. The current-passport requirement is for all pages. A separate entry requests the old passport's bio page and current visa page. Obtain instructions if an old passport is unavailable. (PDF p. 8)

### Q68. Which police certificates do I need?
**Summary states:** For applicants aged 16+, certificates are requested from every country lived in for more than six months over the last ten years, plus the country of birth and country of residence. The summary does not define continuous versus cumulative residence or certificate validity. (PDF p. 8)

### Q69. My child is 16. Do they need a police certificate as well as an interview?
**Summary states:** Yes, both stated thresholds include age 16. These are different requirements. (PDF pp. 4, 8)

### Q70. What medical documents are listed for children?
**Summary states:** Blood and urine tests from age five, HIV results for applicants over 12, and immunization records for all applicants. Test results must be no older than three months. The wording for age 12 needs confirmation; approved clinics and the effect of results on eligibility are not explained. (PDF p. 8)

### Q71. Does a medical result automatically disqualify someone?
**Not covered:** The checklist asks for tests but does not explain acceptance criteria, exemptions or consequences. Obtain qualified case-specific guidance rather than diagnosing eligibility from the checklist.

### Q72. What banking and employment evidence do I need?
**Summary states:** The main applicant needs twelve months of bank statements, an original bank reference and an original professional reference. Employees need an original employment letter; company owners need certified business-license or company documents. Minimum balances and reference wording are not provided. (PDF p. 8)

### Q73. Can someone else sponsor my investment?
**Clarification required:** The checklist includes a support affidavit when the applicant is sponsored by another person, but does not define permitted sponsors, funding sources or approval conditions. Obtain the sponsorship and source-of-funds requirements before accepting a funding arrangement. (PDF p. 8)

### Q74. Do the documents need translation or an apostille?
**Summary states:** The brochure says documents issued in Hague Convention member countries require an apostille. **Not covered:** Translation rules, accepted certifiers, exceptions and the authentication route for non-member countries. Request a country-specific document checklist. (PDF p. 8)

### Q75. How recent must my documents be?
**Summary states:** Address proof, blood/urine results and HIV results must be no older than three months; photos must have been taken within six months. No universal validity period is given for other documents. Coordinate collection with the submission timeline. (PDF p. 8)

### Q76. What photo size and number should I order?
**Clarification required:** Eight photos per applicant are requested, but the photo instructions list two sizes: approximately **45 × 38 mm** and **63.5 × 50.8 mm**. The split between sizes is not explained. Confirm the quantity of each size before ordering. (PDF p. 8)

### Q77. Can I wear glasses or a hijab in the photographs?
**Summary states:** Sunglasses are not permitted; tinted prescription glasses may be worn if the eyes remain visible. The summary says a hijab may be worn but the ears must be visible. Obtain the currently accepted specification for the applicant rather than importing another country's photo rules. (PDF p. 8)

### Q78. What other photo rules are given?
**Summary states:** Front-facing close view of head and shoulders; head occupies 70–80%; eyes open and directed at the camera; no hair over eyes; neutral expression with closed mouth; plain white background; sharp, clear, unaltered color image on high-quality paper. Photos should be identical in all aspects and taken within six months. (PDF p. 8)

### Q79. What if I do not have a driver's license, military certificate or education document?
**Clarification required:** The military certificate is expressly conditional; a no-license or missing-education-document procedure is not described. Obtain an exemption or alternative-document instruction for the specific item rather than omitting it silently. (PDF p. 8)

### Q80. Is this checklist final?
**Summary states:** No. The Citizenship Investment Unit (CIU), as named in the checklist, may request additional documents or information. The brochure does not give the full official application sequence, appeal procedure or direct-submission rules. (PDF p. 8)

## 8. Travel, tax and life after approval

### Q81. How many countries will I be able to visit?
**Clarification required:** The overview says visa-free travel to 140 countries, while the benefits page says visa-free and visa-on-arrival travel to 143. The categories and counts differ. There is no destination list in this PDF, so no current country-by-country promise can be made from it. (PDF pp. 2, 5)

### Q82. Does it include the EU, UK, US or Canada?
**Summary states:** The brochure broadly mentions EU members, without listing them or giving entry conditions. **Not covered:** Specific access to the UK, US or Canada. Verify the customer's intended destination, purpose and duration separately; do not infer access from a headline count. (PDF p. 5)

### Q83. Can I live and work anywhere in CARICOM?
**Clarification required:** The brochure claims unrestricted rights to live, work, travel and study throughout CARICOM, without conditions or implementation details. Verify the particular country and activity before treating this marketing statement as an unconditional personal entitlement. (PDF p. 5)

### Q84. Does visa-free access let me settle or work in Europe?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not establish foreign employment, study or permanent-residence rights from travel access. Ask what the customer intends to do and obtain the destination's applicable requirements.

### Q85. Will I pay no tax after getting citizenship?
**Clarification required:** The brochure says “no taxes on non-residents,” but does not define tax residence, local-source income, specific taxes or obligations elsewhere. Citizenship alone does not answer the customer's personal tax question. A move to Dominica may also change the assumptions behind a non-resident claim. Obtain an individual assessment. (PDF pp. 2, 5)

### Q86. Can my children inherit citizenship?

## 11. Consultation workflow

**Trainer-created workflow, not the official application process.**

1. **Define the goal:** Relocation, travel flexibility, family planning, education or another specific objective. Record destination and activity if the goal is outside Dominica.
2. **Map the household:** Relationships, birth dates, citizenships, residence history, student status, disability/support needs and marital status.
3. **Screen unresolved eligibility:** Exact ages 18, 30 and 65; adult children; restricted origins or nationalities; previous legal or immigration issues; source of funds.
4. **Compare the two routes:** Contribution versus property, required cash, fee differences, investment terms and liquidity needs.
5. **Prepare a written estimate:** Separate investment, government fees, due diligence, interviews, program charges and unquoted costs. Label every assumption.
6. **Plan documents:** Assign an owner, issuing country, certification method and expiry check to each required item.
7. **Resolve questions:** Obtain current written eligibility, process, interview, payment and property-exit instructions from the responsible case team.
8. **Confirm next steps:** Engagement scope, case contact, payment milestones, document schedule and realistic timing.

### Discovery prompts

- What is the main problem you want a second citizenship to solve?
- Are you planning to live in Dominica or primarily use the passport for travel?
- Who should apply now, and who might be added later?
- Are adult children enrolled in higher education and substantially supported?
- Are parents or grandparents above the stated age threshold and substantially supported?
- What citizenships, places of birth and residence histories does each applicant have?
- What is the source of the investment money, and is a sponsor involved?
- What total budget and liquidity requirements do you have?
- Would you prefer a non-refundable contribution or an asset purchase with ownership costs?
- Is a school term, family event or business deadline driving the timeline?
- What schooling, healthcare, accessibility or employment needs would a relocation involve?

## 13. Role-play exercises

### Scenario A: A four-person family wants the lowest price

**Customer:** “We are a couple with children aged 10 and 17. I thought the price was $200,000.”

**Expected answer:** Explain the $250,000 stated family donation and separate fees. Use the $271,990 training subtotal only with all assumptions and exclusions. Explain that the 17-year-old triggers interview and due-diligence charges.

**Challenge:** “Can you call that all-inclusive?” The answer must identify unquoted professional costs and unconfirmed charging units.

### Scenario B: Couple without children

**Customer:** “We are just two adults. Tell me the exact donation.”

**Expected answer:** Identify the missing two-person donation amount. Request a written quote instead of extrapolating from single or family-of-four figures. The property table has a family-of-up-to-four government-fee line, but that does not fill the EDF gap.

### Scenario C: Adult child and parent at boundary ages

**Customer:** “My student daughter is 30 and my mother is 65. Can they both join?”

**Expected answer:** Flag both age issues, confirm education and substantial support, and obtain written eligibility decisions. Do not use a fee table to prove eligibility.

### Scenario D: Iranian family

**Customer:** “We are a couple with children aged 13 and 17. Does the normal fee schedule apply?”

**Expected answer:** Explain the special schedule and calculate $65,000 enhanced due diligence alone. Do not assume a free interview, current eligibility or a complete total from that component.

### Scenario E: Property buyer wants an exit guarantee

**Customer:** “I will invest $200,000, recover it in three years and pay no extra charges.”

**Expected answer:** Explain property government fees, other charges and the 4% line. Distinguish the brochure's resale statement from guaranteed repayment or a buyer. Obtain current holding/resale rules and contract terms.

### Scenario F: Relocating for work and tax reasons

**Customer:** “I want to work in another Caribbean country and stop paying taxes everywhere.”

**Expected answer:** Ask for the country, work activity, existing residence and citizenships. Flag the broad CARICOM and non-resident tax claims as insufficient for the proposed plan. Arrange the relevant individual assessments without promising unrestricted work or worldwide tax exemption.
