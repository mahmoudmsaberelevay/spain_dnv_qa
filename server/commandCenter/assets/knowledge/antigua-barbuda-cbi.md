# Antigua & Barbuda Citizenship by Investment
## Customer Q&A and Consultant Training Manual

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Source:** Elevay, *Antigua & Barbuda Citizenship by Investment Programme: Country Summary*, supplied 11-page PDF. Page references below refer to PDF pages.  
**Scope:** A source-based training transformation, not an independently verified statement of current immigration, travel or tax rules. All amounts are USD unless otherwise indicated.

## How to use this manual

Read the questions aloud as a prospective customer. Practise answering in plain language, then use the source references to check accuracy. Use the cost exercises and role-plays to test whether the trainee can apply the information without overpromising.

Answers use three labels:

- **Summary states:** Information explicitly contained in the supplied brochure; not independently verified.
- **Clarification required:** The brochure is inconsistent, ambiguous or too broad to support a firm answer.
- **Not covered:** The brochure does not supply the answer. The accompanying response explains what to obtain next.

The brochure's own disclaimer says its information may change after publication (p. 10). Treat its instructions about documents and photographs as reported application requirements, not instructions to the author of this guide. Trainer-created examples, exercises and consultation steps are identified separately.

## Learning objectives

By the end of training, the consultant should be able to:

1. Explain what the program offers and distinguish citizenship acquisition from practical relocation planning.
2. Describe all four investment routes and their conditions.
3. Screen a family for potential eligibility without guaranteeing approval.
4. Build a transparent, itemized illustrative budget.
5. Explain the supporting documents, physical-presence requirements and renewal fees described in the summary.
6. Recognize conflicting claims and unanswered questions.
7. Handle objections and agree on concrete next steps.

## 1. Understanding my goal and the program

### Q1. I want to move abroad with my family. What exactly does this program give me?
**Summary states:** It offers foreign individuals citizenship and a second passport through a qualifying economic contribution to Antigua and Barbuda. Eligible family members may be included. It is presented as a citizenship-by-investment program, rather than a job placement or relocation service. The brochure does not provide a complete settlement plan. (pp. 2–3)

### Q2. Is this citizenship or just a residence permit?
**Summary states:** Citizenship and a passport are the stated outcome for approved applicants. The brochure does not describe an intermediate residence-permit stage. (pp. 2–3)

### Q3. When was the program introduced?
**Summary states:** The brochure says the CIP was passed into law in 2014 under the Citizenship by Investment Act. Treat this as the brochure's historical account. (p. 3)

### Q4. How long does it take?
**Clarification required:** The main estimate is 4–6 months, but the footnote refers to 3–6 months and warns that additional due diligence can cause longer processing. Say: “The brochure gives an indicative 4–6 months, but we need a current case-specific estimate; it is not a guaranteed deadline.” The starting point for that estimate is not defined. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q5. Can you guarantee approval or a passport by my travel date?
**Not covered:** No approval guarantee or binding delivery date is provided. Additional due diligence may delay processing. Do not commit the client to travel or relocation dates on the basis of this estimate. (p. 2)

### Q6. Can I keep my existing citizenship?
**Summary states:** Antigua and Barbuda permits dual citizenship. The summary does not establish whether the client's existing country permits it or imposes reporting or other conditions. Check that separately. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q7. Do I need to speak English or pass a language test?
**Summary states:** No language requirement is listed. English is described as the official language of the country. (pp. 2, 4, 7)

### Q8. Do I need a degree or business-management experience?
**Summary states:** The benefits page says there are no education or management requirements. However, the document checklist requests education certificates for adult applicants. “No education qualification requirement” does not mean “no education documents.” (pp. 4, 10)

### Q9. Is an interview required?
**Clarification required:** Page 4 says there is no interview requirement, but the fee schedules list a $1,500 interview fee. The summary cannot resolve this. Confirm who must attend, the format, the current charge and whether it is per application or per person. (pp. 4–6)

### Q10. Do I have to live there permanently?
**Summary states:** The brochure describes a minimum stay of five days in a five-year period. It does not require permanent relocation in its description. The exact counting rules, applicable period and evidence required are not explained. (pp. 2, 4)

## 2. Can my family qualify?

### Q11. Can my spouse apply with me?
**Summary states:** The main applicant's current spouse is an eligible dependent category. (p. 3)

### Q12. Can I include my children?
**Clarification required:** The overview refers to dependent children “under 30,” while the detailed list says a child of the main applicant or spouse aged “30 years or younger.” Confirm eligibility for a child exactly 30 and what evidence of dependency is required. (pp. 2–4)

### Q13. Can I include my spouse's child from a previous relationship?
**Summary states:** The detailed list includes children of the main applicant or spouse. Custody, consent, adoption and stepchild-document requirements are not explained. (p. 3)

### Q14. My adult child has a disability. Is there an age limit?
**Summary states:** A child of the applicant or spouse of any age may be included if physically or mentally incapacitated and fully supported by the applicant, using the brochure's terminology. The required medical evidence and assessment process are not specified. (p. 3)

### Q15. Can my parents or grandparents join?
**Clarification required:** The detailed list includes financially dependent parents or grandparents of the applicant or spouse aged 55 or older. Other pages say “over 55.” Confirm the exact age boundary and required proof of financial dependency. (pp. 2–4)

### Q16. Can I include my brother or sister?
**Summary states:** The detailed rule describes an unmarried sibling of the main applicant or spouse who is below 18, with parental or guardian consent. The overview's shorter reference to “unmarried siblings” should not be used to promise eligibility to an adult sibling. (pp. 2–3)

### Q17. Can my spouse's parents or siblings be included?
**Summary states:** Yes, those relationships appear in the detailed categories, subject to the applicable age, dependency, unmarried-status and consent conditions. (p. 3)

### Q18. What if I marry after receiving citizenship?
**Summary states:** A future spouse of the main applicant is listed, with a $50,000 fee payable upon application. The brochure does not establish whether that is the complete cost after all other applicable fees. (p. 3)

### Q19. Can my dependent child add a future spouse?
**Summary states:** A future spouse of a dependent child is listed where the child remains financially dependent on the main applicant. The detailed procedure and fee treatment are not supplied. (p. 3)

### Q20. Can grandchildren be added later?
**Clarification required:** A future child of a dependent child is listed. Page 3 gives $10,000 for children under five and $20,000 for ages 6–17; page 5 gives $10,000 for ages 0–5 and $25,000 for ages 6–17 in its later-addition schedule. Confirm the applicable category, age-five treatment and fee before quoting. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q21. Is there a deadline to add dependents?
**Summary states:** Page 5 says there is no time restriction to add a dependent to an existing application, including after approval. Eligibility, documentation and additional fees still need confirmation for the particular person. (p. 5)

### Q22. What fees does the brochure list for later additions?
**Clarification required:** Page 5 lists $10,000 for a dependent child aged 0–5, $25,000 for ages 6–17 and $50,000 for dependent adults aged 18 or above. Its child figures differ from page 3. Do not present them as a reconciled or all-inclusive schedule. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q23. Do I need a clean criminal record?
**Summary states:** Applicants must hold a clean criminal record from their country of origin. The document checklist is broader: adult applicants need police certificates from all citizenship countries and countries where they lived for more than six months during the preceding ten years. (pp. 4, 10)

### Q24. Are any nationalities restricted?
**Summary states:** The brochure names Iran, Somalia, Yemen, Afghanistan, North Korea and Sudan. It describes possible exemptions after at least ten years living in a non-restricted country with no economic ties to the restricted country. This is not enough to determine any individual's eligibility; obtain a current assessment. (p. 4)

### Q25. What are the minimum age, minimum income and minimum net worth for the main applicant?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not state these thresholds. Do not invent them from the investment amount or dependent rules. Obtain current eligibility criteria before screening a client out or accepting the case.

### Q26. What if I have a previous visa refusal, legal issue or complicated source of funds?
**Not covered:** The summary does not explain their effect on eligibility. Obtain a complete account and supporting evidence for a case-specific review before making assurances.

## 3. Which investment route should I consider?

### Q27. What are my four choices?
**Summary states:** The routes are the National Development Fund, higher education, approved real estate and an approved business investment. Headline figures are not total application costs. (pp. 3, 5–6)

| Route | Brochure investment/contribution | Key qualification in the brochure |
|---|---:|---|
| National Development Fund (NDF) | $230,000 for 1–4 people; $245,000 for 5 or more | Non-refundable contribution; processing and other fees additional |
| Higher education | $260,000 | Family of 6 or more; processing included for first 6 |
| Approved real estate | $300,000 individual; $200,000 each for two related-party applicants | Approved project; five-year holding requirement; other joint arrangement described below |
| Approved business | $1,500,000 individual; at least $400,000 per investor in a qualifying joint investment | Joint investment must total at least $5,000,000 and involve at least two investors |

### Q28. What is the National Development Fund contribution used for?
**Summary states:** The NDF supports development, infrastructure and a sustainable future for Antigua and Barbuda. The payment is non-refundable. (p. 3)

### Q29. How much is the NDF contribution for my family?
**Summary states:** The detailed schedule lists $230,000 for one to four people and $245,000 for five or more. Additional processing and other charges must be added. (p. 5)

### Q30. Will I get my NDF money back later?
**Summary states:** No repayment is described: the contribution is explicitly non-refundable. The brochure does not explain the timing of payment or what happens to each payment if an application is refused or withdrawn. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q31. Who can use the higher-education route?
**Summary states:** A family of six or more may invest $260,000 into an approved higher-education institution. Processing fees for six are included; additional dependents or siblings beyond six attract $10,000 each in processing fees. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q32. Does this route pay for my child's university degree?
**Summary states:** It entitles one family member to one year of tuition. The summary does not identify the institution, eligible courses, admission criteria, deadline to use the benefit, living costs or coverage of later academic years. Do not promise a fully funded degree. (p. 3)

### Q33. Is the education payment refundable or income-producing?
**Not covered:** The summary calls it an investment but supplies no repayment, return, liquidity or refund terms. Obtain the governing terms before describing it as recoverable capital. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q34. Can I buy any property I like?
**Summary states:** The investment must be in an approved project. The brochure mentions villas, condominiums, commercial developments and luxury resorts; it does not supply a project list. (pp. 3, 6)

### Q35. What is the individual real-estate investment amount?
**Summary states:** $300,000 into an approved real-estate project, plus applicable processing and other fees. (pp. 3, 6)

### Q36. Can I share a property investment with someone else?
**Summary states:** Two related-party applications may each invest at least $200,000 in an approved project. A separate provision describes two or more applicants with a binding sale-and-purchase agreement, each contributing at least $300,000. Confirm the relationship definition and legal structure; these are not interchangeable price options. (p. 6)

### Q37. When may I sell the property?
**Summary states:** The investment must be held for at least five years following the grant of citizenship. Page 6 says units or shares can then be sold to subsequent program applicants as eligible investments, but may only be resold once under the program. That is not a promise of a buyer or sale price. (pp. 4, 6)

### Q38. Can I rent it out, live in it or receive a guaranteed return?
**Not covered:** Occupancy rights, rental arrangements, returns, maintenance fees, taxes, insurance, developer guarantees and exit arrangements are not specified. Obtain the project contract and an itemized ownership-cost schedule.

### Q39. Can I qualify for the business route with $400,000 on my own?
**Summary states:** The individual route requires at least $1,500,000. The $400,000 figure is a minimum per investor within a qualifying joint business investment totaling at least $5,000,000. (pp. 3, 6)

### Q40. Would two investors contributing $400,000 each qualify?
**Summary states:** Not under the stated joint-investment conditions: $800,000 is below the $5,000,000 total. Both the individual minimum and aggregate minimum must be met. (p. 6)

### Q41. Must I personally run the business or create a set number of jobs?
**Not covered:** The brochure says the business must be approved, but does not define sectors, job-creation rules, operating duties, ownership structure or holding period. Its general “no management requirements” claim is not a substitute for business-route terms. (pp. 4, 6)

### Q42. Which route is best for me?
**Trainer guidance:** First establish family size, ages, budget, need for recoverable capital, willingness to own property or a business, and interest in the tuition benefit. Compare full written costs and terms. The brochure alone cannot establish the best option for a particular client.

## 4. What will I actually pay?

### Q43. Is the advertised investment my total cost?
**Summary states:** No. The tables also show processing, due diligence, passport, interview, renewal and professional fees. Professional fees are available on request. (pp. 5–6)

### Q44. What processing fees apply to NDF, real estate and business?
**Summary states:** $10,000 for a single applicant; $20,000 for a family of up to four; and $10,000 for each additional dependent or sibling beyond four. The single-applicant line should be used for a one-person illustration. (pp. 5–6)

### Q45. What processing fees apply to higher education?
**Summary states:** The $260,000 includes processing for six family members. Each additional dependent or sibling beyond six costs $10,000 in processing fees. Other fee categories are still listed separately. (p. 5)

### Q46. What are the due-diligence charges?
**Summary states:** The same schedule is printed for all four routes. (pp. 5–6)

| Person/category | Listed due-diligence fee |
|---|---:|
| Main applicant | $8,500 |
| Spouse | $5,000 |
| Dependent or sibling aged 0–11 | $0 |
| Dependent or sibling aged 12–17 | $2,000 each |
| Dependent or sibling aged 18+ | $4,000 each |
| Dependent parent aged 55+ | $4,000 each |
| Benefactor | $5,000 each |

**Clarification required:** A fee row is not proof of eligibility. The adult-sibling row does not resolve the under-18 eligibility rule. Grandparents are eligible in the family description but not separately named in this fee table; confirm their charge.

### Q47. What is a benefactor, and can someone else fund my application?
**Not covered:** A $5,000 due-diligence fee per benefactor is listed, but the term and permitted funding arrangements are not defined. Confirm whether third-party funding is permitted and what relationship, source-of-funds and payment evidence is required. (pp. 5–6)

### Q48. What other initial fees are listed?
**Summary states:** A $300 passport fee per person and a $1,500 interview fee. The charging unit for the interview is not defined. Professional fees are quoted on request. (pp. 5–6)

### Q49. What renewal fees should I budget for?
**Summary states:** Under “5-year renewal application fees,” the brochure lists $1,000 per person aged 18 or above and $500 per person aged 17 or below. It does not give a full renewal process or explain whether additional charges apply. (pp. 5–6)

### Q50. Are legal, translation, property and travel costs included?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not give a complete schedule for those items. Request an itemized quote covering any applicable professional charges, document certification, translation, authentication, transfers, travel and investment-specific costs. Do not describe an illustration as an all-in price.

### Q51. When must I transfer the money, and who receives it?
**Not covered:** Payment milestones, account details, escrow, payment methods and refund rules are not supplied. Obtain written payment instructions and terms before any transfer.

### Q52. Can I borrow the investment or pay in installments?
**Not covered:** Financing and installment eligibility are not explained. Do not promise either arrangement without confirmation.

### Q53. What happens to my money if I am rejected or withdraw?
**Not covered:** The NDF contribution is described as non-refundable, but the brochure does not provide a complete refusal/withdrawal policy for contributions, investments or fees. Obtain route-specific written terms, including when each liability arises.

## 5. Worked cost examples

**Trainer-created calculations using brochure figures, not quotations.** Assume all named dependents qualify, no benefactor, and **one $1,500 interview charge per application purely for calculation practice**. That interview assumption requires confirmation. Exclude professional fees, renewal, travel, document costs, transaction costs and other unlisted charges. Do not use these subtotals as guaranteed current prices.

| Example | Investment/contribution | Processing | Due diligence | Passports | Assumed interview | Illustrative subtotal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A. Single applicant, NDF | $230,000 | $10,000 | $8,500 | $300 | $1,500 | **$250,300** |
| B. Couple + children aged 8 and 15, NDF | $230,000 | $20,000 | $15,500 | $1,200 | $1,500 | **$268,200** |
| C. Couple + children aged 8, 15 and 20, NDF | $245,000 | $30,000 | $19,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 | **$297,500** |
| D. Couple + children aged 8, 15, 20 and 24, higher education | $260,000 | Included for 6 | $23,500 | $1,800 | $1,500 | **$286,800** |
| E. Same six-person family as D, NDF | $245,000 | $40,000 | $23,500 | $1,800 | $1,500 | **$311,800** |
| F. Single applicant, individual real estate | $300,000 | $10,000 | $8,500 | $300 | $1,500 | **$320,300** |
| G. Single applicant, individual business | $1,500,000 | $10,000 | $8,500 | $300 | $1,500 | **$1,520,300** |

### Q54. Why is the higher-education example cheaper than NDF for that six-person family?
**Calculation:** The education route includes processing for six, while NDF adds $40,000 processing for that family. On the stated assumptions, the education subtotal is $25,000 lower. This does not establish which route is better after verifying terms and all costs.

### Q55. What would a seventh family member add to the education example?
**Calculation:** For an eligible additional dependent aged 10, the listed marginal fees would be $10,000 processing, $0 due diligence and $300 passport: $10,300, before any other applicable charges. For an eligible additional dependent aged 20, the same calculation is $14,300. Confirm whether interview or other fees change.

### Q56. How do I build a reliable budget for my own family?
**Trainer guidance:** List every applicant's relationship and age, verify eligibility, select the route, calculate the base contribution/investment, add route-specific processing, add each person's due diligence and passport charge, confirm interview charging, then add professional and other applicable costs. Keep initial costs and later renewal costs separate. Record assumptions and quote validity in writing.

### Q58. Do you need my full passport or only the photo page?
**Summary states:** All pages of the current passport are requested. The old-passport entry separately asks for the bio page and current visa page. Clarify missing or expired passports rather than silently omitting the entry. (p. 10)

### Q59. How recent must my proof of address be?
**Summary states:** No older than three months. The list names the main applicant and dependents aged 18 or above who do not live with the main applicant. Confirm acceptable documents and whether two different proofs are required. (p. 10)

### Q60. How much banking history is required?
**Summary states:** Twelve months of bank statements and an original bank reference letter for the main applicant. The summary does not define minimum balances, account types or reference wording. (p. 10)

### Q61. Which police certificates do I need if I have lived abroad?
**Summary states:** For applicants aged 18 or above, certificates from every country of citizenship and each country where they lived for more than six months during the past ten years. The summary does not define whether the six months must be continuous or cumulative or give certificate validity periods. (p. 10)

### Q62. Are medical tests required for children?
**Summary states:** The checklist requests an original HIV result no older than three months for all applicants aged five or above. It does not explain approved providers, other medical tests, exemptions or the effect of a result on eligibility. (p. 10)

### Q63. Do adult dependents need evidence of financial support?
**Summary states:** The checklist requests an original signed sworn support affidavit for each dependent over 18, excluding the spouse. Elevay supplies the draft. The age wording should be checked for a dependent exactly 18. (p. 10)

### Q64. Do documents need an apostille or translation?
**Summary states:** The brochure says documents issued by countries belonging to the Hague Convention require an apostille. **Not covered:** Translation requirements, accepted certifiers and the process for non-member countries. Obtain a document-by-document authentication checklist. (p. 10)

### Q65. What photo specifications should I give the photographer?
**Summary states:** Eight identical, recent passport-sized photos per applicant, taken within six months. Approximately 35 × 45 mm, color, high-quality paper, plain white background, sharp and unaltered. Show a close front view of head and shoulders with the head occupying 70–80% of the image; eyes open, looking at the camera, ears visible, no hair over eyes, no glasses or sunglasses, neutral expression and closed mouth. The brochure says nothing should cover part of the head; exceptions are not explained. (p. 10)

### Q66. Can immigration ask for anything else?
**Summary states:** Yes. The Citizenship Investment Unit (CIU), as named in the brochure, may request additional documents or information not listed. (p. 10)

### Q67. What are the official application stages?
**Not covered:** The summary does not give a complete submission, due-diligence, approval, investment-payment, oath and passport-issuance sequence. Obtain the current process map, responsibilities and payment milestones.

### Q68. Can I apply directly, or must I use an authorized agent?
**Not covered:** The brochure markets Elevay's services but does not state the legal rules on direct submission or identify an authorized submitting agent. Confirm the submission arrangement and service contract.

### Q69. Can I appeal a refusal or correct a rejected document?
**Not covered:** Appeal, review, resubmission and document-correction procedures are not supplied. Request the applicable process and any deadlines before giving advice.

## 7. Travel, presence and life after approval

### Q70. Do my children need to complete the five-day stay?
**Summary states:** Dependent children under 18 are described as exempt from visiting. On reaching 18, the brochure says they must satisfy the residency requirement and swear an oath or pledge allegiance. It does not state the deadline after the eighteenth birthday. (p. 4)

### Q71. Must the five days be consecutive, and how is presence proved?
**Not covered:** The summary does not define consecutive versus accumulated days, arrival/departure counting, proof or the consequences of non-compliance. Obtain the applicable instructions. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q72. Must I travel to Antigua for the oath?
**Clarification required:** A note under the business route says the applicant must go to Antigua for the oath of naturalization after approval. The brochure does not establish whether this instruction applies identically to other routes or whether alternative venues exist. (p. 6)

### Q73. How long is the passport valid, and can citizenship expire?
**Not covered:** Five-year renewal application fees are listed, but the summary does not fully define passport validity by age, citizenship duration, revocation grounds or later renewal conditions. Do not equate a passport renewal fee with renewal of citizenship. (pp. 5–6)

### Q74. How many countries can I visit with the passport?
**Clarification required:** The overview says over 150; page 4 says over 153; page 8 is headed 153. Page 8 also includes visa-on-arrival destinations. Present this as the brochure's travel-access claim, not a verified count of strictly visa-free destinations. (pp. 2, 4, 8)

### Q75. Does the summary include the UK, Schengen area and Hong Kong?
**Summary states:** Yes, it highlights them. It does not give permitted stay lengths, travel purposes, electronic authorization requirements or entry conditions. Verify the intended destination before advising travel. (pp. 2, 8)

### Q76. Does this let me move to the UK or Europe permanently?
**Not covered:** The summary mentions travel access but provides no basis to promise permanent residence, employment or study rights in the UK or Europe. Obtain destination-specific rules for the client's actual purpose.

### Q77. Can I live and work anywhere in CARICOM?
**Clarification required:** Page 4 claims an unrestricted right to live, work, travel and study throughout CARICOM. It supplies no treaty conditions, categories, implementation details or exceptions. Do not repeat that as an unconditional personal entitlement; verify the proposed destination and activity.

### Q78. Does it give visa-free access to the US or Canada?
**Not covered:** Neither is included in the brochure's destination list. The summary provides no basis to promise visa-free entry or another immigration benefit there. (p. 8)

### Q79. Will I stop paying tax on worldwide income?
**Clarification required:** The summary claims “no tax on worldwide income,” but does not discuss tax residence, source of income, other citizenships, existing tax obligations, local taxes or reporting. It cannot determine the client's personal tax position. Obtain a qualified assessment before making a tax plan. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q80. Can I pass citizenship to future generations automatically?
**Not covered:** The brochure describes some later family additions but does not establish automatic citizenship by descent, birth registration rules or all transmission conditions. Do not infer automatic inheritance from dependent-addition eligibility.

## 8. What is living there actually like?

### Q81. Where is the country, and what is the capital?
**Summary states:** It is in the Eastern Caribbean; its capital is Saint John's and its area is given as 440 km². (p. 7)

### Q82. What language, time zone and government does it have?
**Summary states:** English is official, with local dialects; the time zone is UTC−4. The government is described as a parliamentary democracy under a constitutional monarchy. (p. 7)

### Q83. What currency will I use?
**Summary states:** Eastern Caribbean dollars (XCD), described as pegged to USD, with a rate of USD 1 to XCD 2.70. No banking, exchange-service or card fees are provided. (p. 7)

### Q84. What does the brochure say about the lifestyle?
**Summary states:** It emphasizes warm temperatures throughout the year, coastline, beaches, tourism and access to the Caribbean economy. It recounts Nelson's establishment of a British Caribbean base in 1784 and mentions Oprah Winfrey, Eric Clapton and Robert De Niro in its lifestyle marketing. These points do not establish current living costs or service quality. (pp. 4, 7)

### Q85. How much will rent, groceries, transport and utilities cost my family?
**Not covered:** No household budget or current prices are supplied. Establish household size, neighborhood, housing standard, schooling and transport needs, then obtain a separate relocation budget.

### Q86. What schools and universities are available?
**Not covered:** The summary does not list schools, curriculum choices, admissions, tuition or special-education support. The investment route's one-year tuition benefit is not a general education-system guide.

### Q87. What healthcare and insurance will we need?
**Not covered:** Hospitals, insurance eligibility, premiums, specialist care and emergency evacuation are not discussed. Obtain a healthcare plan based on family needs.

### Q88. Can you help me find a job or open a business after moving?
**Not covered:** Job placement, salaries, recruitment, local licensing and business setup outside the investment route are not described. Confirm the service scope separately.

### Q89. Can I open a bank account or get a mortgage?
**Not covered:** Citizenship-related bank documents are listed, but account-opening eligibility, lending, interest rates and banking services after relocation are not covered.

### Q90. What about safety, hurricanes, transport, internet and flights?
**Not covered:** The summary does not give evidence on these practical issues. Prepare a separate, current relocation briefing for the intended home location rather than treating tourism language as the answer.

## 9. Who will support my application?

### Q91. Who is Elevay?
**Summary states:** Elevay describes itself as a Dubai-based international citizenship and residency planning organization with over 25 years of experience and hundreds of satisfied clients. It says its name is inspired by the French word “Elevé,” meaning to rise or elevate. These are the firm's own claims. (p. 9)

### Q92. What services does it say it provides?
**Summary states:** Document-processing systems, a network of legal, immigration and real-estate professionals, and personalized client support. Its “zero margin for error” wording is marketing language; the brochure provides no contractual guarantee of an error-free process or approval. (p. 9)

## 11. Consultation workflow and discovery checklist

**Trainer-created workflow; not an official application sequence.**

1. **Define the goal:** Relocation to Antigua and Barbuda, travel flexibility, family planning, education, business or another specific purpose.
2. **Map the family:** Names, relationships, dates of birth, marital status, dependency, citizenships and countries of residence. Identify any boundary ages.
3. **Check potential obstacles:** Nationality restrictions, residence history, criminal records, previous immigration issues, custody/consent and funding source. Route uncertain cases to the qualified case team.
4. **Discuss investment preferences:** Available budget, contribution versus asset ownership, liquidity needs, family size and route conditions.
5. **Build the cost sheet:** Separate investment from fees; itemize people and assumptions; obtain missing professional and transaction costs.
6. **Plan documents:** Use the 19-item checklist; assign an owner and expiry check to each item.
7. **Explain obligations:** Presence, oath, property holding, renewal and case-specific requirements once confirmed.
8. **Record unresolved questions:** Assign a responsible person and written response before any promise or payment instruction.
9. **Confirm next steps:** Written eligibility assessment, route comparison, engagement terms, document checklist and realistic timeline.

## 13. Role-play exercises

### Scenario A: Family of four focused on price

**Customer:** “We are a married couple with children aged eight and fifteen. I thought $230,000 covered everything.”

**Expected response:** Explain NDF contribution versus fees. Calculate $268,200 under the stated exercise assumptions. Identify professional and unlisted costs, verify the interview charge, and avoid calling it a final quote.

**Follow-up challenge:** “Can we get the investment back?” Explain that NDF is described as non-refundable; do not extend that answer to every fee or another route.

### Scenario B: Family of six comparing routes

**Customer:** “We have children aged eight, fifteen, twenty and twenty-four. Should we use the education option?”

**Expected response:** Confirm adult children's eligibility and dependency first. Compare education $286,800 with NDF $311,800 under exercise assumptions. Explain one year of tuition for one family member and identify unanswered education-benefit terms.

### Scenario C: Age boundaries and siblings

**Customer:** “My child is 30, my father is 55 and my unmarried sister is 22. The brochure says family is included.”

**Expected response:** Flag the child and parent boundary conflicts. Explain the detailed sibling rule says below 18; the adult fee row does not prove eligibility. Seek written determinations instead of promising inclusion.

### Scenario D: Property investor

**Customer:** “I will buy a $300,000 apartment and sell it when the passport arrives.”

**Expected response:** Explain approved-project requirements, the five-year holding period following citizenship, fees, and absence of guaranteed resale. Ask about ownership costs and liquidity needs.

### Scenario E: Passport for regional employment

**Customer:** “I want to move to another CARICOM country for work immediately.”

**Expected response:** Record the destination and employment activity. Identify the brochure's broad regional-rights claim as requiring verification. Do not promise unconditional access to employment or settlement.

### Scenario F: Restricted nationality and urgent timeline

**Customer:** “I hold one of the listed restricted nationalities but have lived elsewhere for twelve years. I need a passport in four months.”

**Expected response:** Explain that the brochure mentions an exemption involving at least ten years in a non-restricted country and no economic ties, but an individual assessment is required. Do not guarantee eligibility or the timeline.
