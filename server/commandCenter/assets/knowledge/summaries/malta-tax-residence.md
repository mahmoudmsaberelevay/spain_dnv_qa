## Program Overview

Malta tax-residence programmes

Malta has tax-residence programmes under which the beneficiary is taxed at 15% on income arising outside Malta that is received in Malta, subject to a minimum tax each year, and must live in a qualifying home in Malta. Which programme applies depends on nationality and situation: the Global Residence Programme for non-EU nationals, the Residence Programme for EU, EEA and Swiss nationals, and the Malta Retirement Programme for pensioners. New rules apply from 1 January 2027 (page 5).

From 1 January 2027

The Individual Tax Programme Rules 2026 (LN 195/2026, Gazette 14 July 2026) apply from 1 January 2027.

Minimum tax is the least amount payable each year, whatever the income received in Malta. No processing time is published, so none is shown. Nothing in this summary guarantees that an application will be approved.

How the 15% and the minimum tax work

Two numbers matter: the 15% rate and the yearly minimum. They work together, they are not alternatives.

- 15% rate. Income arising outside Malta that is received in Malta in the previous year is taxed at 15%.
- Minimum tax. Whatever the amount received in Malta, the tax payable for the year is at least the minimum: EUR 15,000 under the Global Residence and Residence Programmes (for the beneficiary, spouse and certain children), or EUR 7,500 under the Malta Retirement Programme, plus EUR 500 a year for each dependant and special carer.
- So the minimum is a floor, not a cap. The 15% is never limited to EUR 15,000. Once 15% of the income received in Malta is more than the minimum, the tax is the 15% amount.
- The minimum is payable in full in the year status is granted and in the year it ends, and tax paid under the rules is not refundable.
- Income that is not taxed at 15% under these rules is taxed separately at 35%.

| Income received in Malta in the year | 15% of that income | Tax payable (Global Residence or Residence Programme) |
|---|---:|---|
| EUR 40,000 | EUR 6,000 | EUR 15,000 (the minimum) |
| EUR 100,000 | EUR 15,000 | EUR 15,000 |
| EUR 200,000 | EUR 30,000 | EUR 30,000 (the 15% amount) |

The 15% amount passes the EUR 15,000 minimum at EUR 100,000 of income received in Malta (EUR 50,000 for the Malta Retirement Programme minimum of EUR 7,500). This is simple arithmetic of the rule, not a tax calculation, and double taxation relief may apply.

The tax position depends on personal circumstances. Independent tax advice is recommended. Elevay does not give tax advice. This summary makes no tax calculation for any individual.

## Who Can be Included

WHO EACH PROGRAMME IS FOR

- Global Residence Programme: nationals of countries outside the EU, EEA and Switzerland.
- Residence Programme: EU, EEA and Swiss nationals who are not Maltese.
- Malta Retirement Programme: pensioners of any nationality except Maltese, whose pension is at least 75% of their income.

From 1 January 2027

Categories: Global resident; EU, EEA and Swiss resident; retired pensioner; United Nations (UN) pensioner. The full eligibility conditions for each 2027 category are not covered here. This summary will be updated in January 2027.

The beneficiary does not need to speak Maltese: the language condition is the ability to communicate adequately in one of Malta’s official languages, which are Maltese and English. A property must be bought or rented and used as the primary residence.

| Condition | Global Residence Programme | Residence Programme | Malta Retirement Programme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nationality | Not Maltese, EEA or Swiss: a third-country national | EU, EEA or Swiss national, not Maltese | Any nationality except Maltese |
| Income | Stable and regular resources to support the beneficiary and dependants without social assistance | Stable and regular resources to support the beneficiary and dependants without social assistance | A pension, documented and received in Malta, that is at least 75% of chargeable income |
| Insurance | Sickness insurance for all risks across the whole European Union, for the beneficiary and dependants | Sickness insurance for all risks across the whole European Union, for the beneficiary and dependants | Sickness insurance for all risks across the whole European Union, for the beneficiary and dependants |
| Language | Can adequately communicate in one of Malta’s official languages (Maltese or English) | Can adequately communicate in one of Malta’s official languages (Maltese or English) | No language condition in the rules |
| Other | Fit and proper person; valid travel document; not a permanent or long-term resident of Malta; not already under another listed Maltese scheme | Fit and proper person; valid travel document; not a permanent or long-term resident of Malta; not already under another listed Maltese scheme | Fit and proper person; valid travel document; not domiciled in Malta and no intention to become domiciled within 5 years of applying; not under another listed scheme |
| Days in Malta | No minimum number of days is set | No minimum number of days is set | At least 90 days a year, averaged over any 5-year period |

No more than 183 days in any other country in a calendar year.

When the status ends

- The beneficiary becomes a Maltese national, or a permanent or long-term resident of Malta.
- The qualifying property is lost, including by letting or subletting it, or the required insurance lapses.
- The 183-day limit is passed, or (Malta Retirement Programme) the 90-day average is not met.
- The authorities decide the stay is against the public interest, or the tax laws are breached.
