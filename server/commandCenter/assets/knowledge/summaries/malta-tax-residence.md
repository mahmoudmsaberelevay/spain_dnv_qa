PROGRAMME OVERVIEW
Malta tax-residence programmes
Malta has tax-residence programmes under which the beneficiary is taxed at 15% on income arising outside Malta
that is received in Malta, subject to a minimum tax each year, and must live in a qualifying home in Malta. Which
programme applies depends on nationality and situation: the Global Residence Programme for non-EU nationals,
the Residence Programme for EU, EEA and Swiss nationals, and the Malta Retirement Programme for pensioners.
New rules apply from 1 January 2027 (page 5).

HOW THE TAX WORKS
How the 15% and the minimum tax work
Two numbers matter: the 15% rate and the yearly minimum. They work together, they are not alternatives.
e¢ 15% rate. Income arising outside Malta that is received in Malta in the previous year is taxed at 15%.
e Minimum tax. Whatever the amount received in Malta, the tax payable for the year is at least the minimum: EUR 15,000
under the Global Residence and Residence Programmes (for the beneficiary, spouse and certain children), or EUR 7,500
under the Malta Retirement Programme, plus EUR 500 a year for each dependant and special carer.
¢ So the minimum is a floor, not a cap. The 15% is never limited to EUR 15,000. Once 15% of the income received in Malta is
more than the minimum, the tax is the 15% amount.
e The minimum is payable in fullin the year status is granted and in the year it ends, and tax paid under the rules is not
refundable.
e Income that is not taxed at 15% under these rules is taxed separately at 35%.

WHEN THE STATUS ENDS
e The beneficiary becomes a Maltese national, or a permanent or long-term resident of Malta.
e The qualifying property is lost, including by letting or subletting it, or the required insurance lapses.
e The 183-day limit is passed, or (Malta Retirement Programme) the 90-day average is not met.
e The authorities decide the stay is against the public interest, or the tax laws are breached.
