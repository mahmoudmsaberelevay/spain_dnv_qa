## Program Overview

Spain Entrepreneur Residence

Programme details checked against Law 14/2013 (Spanish Official State Gazette) and the Spanish Inclusion Ministry’s Large Companies and Strategic Groups Unit, accessed 15 October 2026.

Spain’s entrepreneur residence authorisation (Articles 68 to 70 of Law 14/2013) is for non-EU nationals over 18 who want to start, run or manage an innovative business activity of special economic interest for Spain. The programme is open. A favourable report on the project is mandatory.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Initial authorisation | A single validity of 3 years (Ministry management criterion 2/2025, Article 69.1). |
| Residence authorisation | Up to 20 days; legal maximum for the authorisation, from electronic filing. If no decision is made in that time, the authorisation is deemed granted by administrative silence (Article 76.1). |
| ENISA report | 10 working days; maximum time for the mandatory report on the project. It is due within 10 working days of the UGE’s request (Article 70). This is a separate step. The two periods are not added together here. |
| Visa from abroad | 10 working days, longer where the Schengen consultation applies (Article 75.5). |
| Investment and jobs | The UGE sets no minimum investment and no minimum job creation. The project is assessed on its merits. |

These are legal maximums, not promises. Nothing in this summary guarantees that an application will be approved. The report is judged on the merits of the project and its outcome cannot be promised.

Who can apply and how the project is judged

| Condition | What the law and the UGE say |
|---|---|
| Who | A non-EU foreign national over 18, entering Spain or already holding a stay, residence or visa authorisation, who wants to start, run or manage a business activity (Articles 68 and 69). |
| The activity | An innovative activity of special economic interest for Spain, supported by a favourable report (Article 70). |
| The mandatory report | Issued by ENISA after the UGE requests it. It must say whether it is favourable or unfavourable, and is due within 10 working days of the request (Article 70). The applicant gives ENISA the profile, business plan and value-added information through the ENISA website. |
| What is assessed | (1) The applicant’s professional profile and involvement, including each partner’s participation. (2) The business plan: project, product or service, market, financing, investment required and funding sources. (3) The value added for the Spanish economy, such as innovation or an investment opportunity. |
| Investment and jobs | The UGE sets no minimum investment and no minimum job creation. The project is assessed on its merits. |
| General conditions | Over 18; no criminal record in Spain or in the countries lived in during the last 5 years; health insurance with an insurer authorised in Spain; sufficient economic resources; fee paid (Article 62.3). |

Examples of innovation

The UGE instruction gives examples a project can use to show innovation: recent public research and development funding, recognised awards, or the Innovative SME seal. These are examples only. The ENISA report may be unfavourable where there are reasonable doubts about reputational, regulatory, ethical or speculative risks.

The amount of economic resources to show is confirmed at proposal stage. “UGE” is the Large Companies and Strategic Groups Unit of the Spanish Inclusion Ministry (Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos).

How to apply, how long it lasts and how long it can take

| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| Step 1: ENISA report | Register on the ENISA website, choose the entrepreneur residence option and complete the questionnaire. ENISA issues a favourable or unfavourable report on the project. |
| Step 2: Paperwork and fee | Prepare the signed application form and the documents on the checklist. Pay the fee through the Ministry’s payment form and keep the receipt reference. |
| Step 3: Filing with the UGE | The file is filed electronically with the UGE, with a digital certificate or Cl@ve, by the applicant or a representative. |

| Stage | What the law and the UGE say |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneur visa | Valid for 1 year, to make preliminary arrangements. It can convert to residence without a new visa once the business has effectively started (Article 68). |
| Initial authorisation | A single validity of 3 years (Ministry management criterion 2/2025, Article 69.1). |
| Renewal | A separate route with the same three steps. The authority checks that the conditions that justified the grant still hold, and renewals run in 2-year periods while they are met (Article 76.2). Renewal is never promised. |
| Residence authorisation processing time | A maximum of 20 days from filing. If no decision is made in that time, the authorisation is deemed granted by administrative silence (Article 76.1). |
| ENISA report processing time | 10 working days from the UGE’s request (Article 70). This is a separate step. The two periods are not added together here. |
| Visa from abroad processing time | 10 working days, longer where the Schengen consultation applies (Article 75.5). |

These are legal maximums, not promises. Nothing in this summary guarantees that an application will be approved. Renewal is never promised: it depends on the conditions still being met.

Social Security before you start

The application includes a signed declaration committing to meet your Social Security obligations before you start any business or professional activity in Spain. Do not start work before you are correctly registered.

Tax: seek your own advice

Elevay does not give tax advice. Tax residence and personal tax treatment are not set by the immigration authority. It is very important that you seek your own independent tax advice before you apply.

## Who Can be Included

| Family member | What the law and the UGE say |
|---|---|
| Spouse or partner | Spouse or person in an analogous affective relationship. An unmarried, unregistered partner shows at least 1 year of continuous cohabitation, unless there are common children. |
| Children | Minor children, and adult children who depend economically on the holder and have not formed their own family unit. Aged 18 to under 26 if they meet the UGE conditions: no income from work or public benefits, and enrolled in a qualifying course or registered as a job-seeker. Aged 26 or over: only with an incapacity that prevents them from being economically independent. |
| Parents and grandparents | Dependent ascendants show economic or physical dependence; over 80 is presumed. Family members may apply with the holder or later. |

Family application: filed by the family member, the holder or the hiring entity; the same fee model applies to holder and dependants. These age and dependence rules come from the UGE family guidance. {FAMDOC}
