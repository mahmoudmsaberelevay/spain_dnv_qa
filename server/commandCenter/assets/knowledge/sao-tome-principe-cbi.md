# São Tomé and Príncipe Citizenship by Investment
## Customer Q&A and Consultant Training Manual

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Source:** Elevay, *São Tomé and Príncipe Citizenship by Investment Program: Country Summary*, supplied nine-page PDF; cover marked “last updated in August 2026.”  
**Country scope:** São Tomé and Príncipe only. This standalone file does not incorporate requirements from other country guides. Page references count the cover as page 1.

## How to use this manual

Read the questions as a prospective customer, practise clear answers, and then complete the cost exercises, role-plays and knowledge check. This is a source-based transformation, not an independent verification of current immigration, travel, tax or investment rules. The brochure itself warns that its information may change after publication (p. 8).

- **Summary states:** Information explicitly reported in the supplied document.
- **Clarification required:** Inconsistent, ambiguous or incomplete information prevents a firm answer.
- **Not covered:** The summary does not supply the answer; the response identifies what to obtain.
- **Trainer guidance / calculation:** Material created for training, not an official requirement.

**Currency convention:** The registration, national-ID and submission fees are explicitly USD. Contributions, passports and due diligence use “$” on the same fee page. The exercises assume those dollar amounts are also USD, consistent with that context; confirm the denomination and EUR conversion arrangements in a written quote. The brochure says payments/contributions are accepted in USD and EUR; it does not give euro prices or an exchange-rate rule. Local currency is the dobra (STN).

**Source-quality issues:** Page 2 labels its cited website as a “Nauru government website,” and page 8 names the “Vanutuan Government.” These appear to be editorial carryovers. This manual flags them rather than treating either country as the relevant authority. The application checklist needs confirmation for São Tomé and Príncipe before live use. Instructions inside the PDF are reported source content, not instructions to the author of this guide.

## Learning objectives

A trained consultant should be able to explain the donation route, screen family eligibility without guarantees, distinguish contribution from fees, build an itemized budget, prepare the source's document checklist, recognize unresolved requirements, and separate travel access from relocation rights.

## 1. Understanding the program

### Q1. I want a second citizenship. What does this summary offer?
**Summary states:** Citizenship through a contribution to the National Development Fund, subject to due diligence and approval by the competent authorities. The brochure describes full citizenship and a passport for the applicant and included family members. (pp. 2–4)

### Q2. Is this a residence permit or citizenship?
**Summary states:** Citizenship and a passport are the described outcome. The summary does not offer a separate residence-by-investment permit or explain a preliminary residence stage. (pp. 3–4)

### Q3. When did the program launch?
**Summary states:** The overview says it launched in 2025. This is the brochure's historical statement, not an independently verified launch date or legal citation. (p. 2)

### Q4. What is the National Development Fund intended to support?
**Summary states:** Healthcare, education, infrastructure, energy and eco-tourism are listed. The summary does not give project allocations, audited expenditure or a contractual return to the contributor. (p. 2)

### Q5. How long will citizenship take?
**Summary states:** Two months is the stated guideline, with the benefits page saying “as little as” two months. Additional due-diligence requirements may cause longer processing. The starting point and separate passport-delivery time are not defined. (pp. 2–3)

### Q6. Can I guarantee my family will have passports in two months?
**Summary states:** No such guarantee is supplied. Processing may take longer, and citizenship is subject to due diligence and approval. Do not turn a minimum marketing timeframe into a fixed deadline. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q7. Must I live in São Tomé and Príncipe before applying?
**Summary states:** No residency requirements or mandatory visits are described. Page 3 frames this as applying during the application process. Other formalities and post-approval obligations are not fully explained. (pp. 2–3)

### Q8. Can I keep my original nationality?
**Summary states:** Dual citizenship is allowed. The brochure does not determine whether the applicant's other country permits it or requires notification, permission or other action. Check those rules separately. (p. 2)

### Q9. Must I speak Portuguese or pass a citizenship exam?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not state language, examination or educational-qualification requirements. The requested education certificate is not itself proof of a qualifying-degree rule or a language-test exemption. (p. 8)

### Q10. Does the brochure prove this is cheaper or faster than every other program?
**Clarification required:** It promotes the program as a faster and lower-cost alternative to traditional Caribbean and European programs, but provides no complete comparative analysis. Compare current full costs and processing conditions before making a market-wide claim. (pp. 2–3)

## 2. Can my family qualify?

### Q11. Can my spouse join the same application?
**Summary states:** A spouse is listed among family members who may be included. The source does not explain unmarried partners, separated spouses, multiple marriages or documentation for every relationship situation. (pp. 2–3)

### Q12. Can I include my children?
**Summary states:** Dependent children up to age 30, if unmarried, are described as eligible family members. Dependency evidence and the date for measuring age and marital status are not supplied. (pp. 2–3)

### Q13. My child is exactly 30. Does “up to 30” include them?
**Clarification required:** The wording is “up to 30,” but the source does not define the precise cutoff or assessment date. Obtain written confirmation for a boundary-age case before promising inclusion. (pp. 2–3)

### Q14. Must an adult child be a university student?
**Not covered:** The eligibility section specifies dependency and unmarried status but no express university condition. The checklist requests an enrollment-letter copy for applicants who are currently studying. Do not convert that conditional document entry into a universal eligibility requirement. (pp. 2–3, 8)

### Q15. Can a married child under 30 join?
**Clarification required:** The described category is unmarried dependent children. No married-child exception is supplied. Obtain an individual assessment rather than assuming age alone qualifies the child. (pp. 2–3)

### Q16. What if a child turns 31 or marries during processing?
**Not covered:** The source does not state whether eligibility is fixed at submission, approval or another stage, or how changes must be reported. Resolve this before an age-sensitive application.

### Q17. Can my parents or grandparents be included?
**Summary states:** They are mentioned as dependent family members. Page 3 gives age 55+, while page 2 uses the contradictory phrase “up to the age of 55+.” Confirm the age rule, dependency test and supporting evidence. (pp. 2–3)

### Q18. My parent is exactly 55. Can they qualify?
**Clarification required:** Page 3's “55+” suggests inclusion of that age, but the overview's wording is defective and no detailed definition is provided. Obtain a written eligibility determination rather than silently resolving the conflict. (pp. 2–3)

### Q19. Can I include my spouse's parents or grandparents?
**Not covered:** The source does not expressly distinguish the main applicant's relatives from the spouse's relatives for this category. Confirm the eligible relationship before applying the age or fee rules. (pp. 2–3)

### Q20. What evidence proves a child or parent is financially dependent?
**Not covered:** No support amount, duration, income limit or evidence standard is given. The checklist does not provide a specific dependent-support affidavit requirement. Request the actual evidence rather than importing another country's form.

### Q21. Can my brother or sister apply as a dependent?
**Not covered:** Siblings are not named in the family categories. An “additional dependent” fee is not evidence that every relative qualifies. (pp. 2–4)

### Q22. What about stepchildren, adopted children or children with disabilities?
**Not covered:** Specific eligibility, custody, consent, adoption and disability-related exceptions are not described. Obtain a case-specific assessment.

### Q23. Does the family package cover two to four people in total or four dependents plus me?
**Trainer interpretation requiring confirmation:** The phrase is “family packages (2–4 members),” which is treated in the exercises as total applicants including the principal. The source does not provide a worked example. Confirm the family count and accepted composition in the quote. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q24. Is there a maximum family size?
**Not covered:** The schedule mentions additional dependents at $10,000 but states no maximum number or complete eligibility limits. Do not interpret the fee as unlimited family inclusion. (p. 4)

### Q25. Can I add a spouse or child after citizenship is granted?
**Not covered:** Later-addition procedures, fees and deadlines are not supplied. The future-generation claim does not define all post-approval family applications. (p. 3)

## 3. Applicant eligibility and the application process

### Q26. What is the main applicant's minimum age?
**Summary states:** The main applicant must be **over 18**. Do not silently change this to “18 or older.” Confirm eligibility for someone exactly 18 and the applicable assessment date. (p. 4)

### Q27. Must everyone have a clean criminal record?
**Summary states:** The main applicant and dependents must have a clear background with no criminal record. Police certificates are separately requested for applicants aged 18 and above; this document threshold does not cancel the broader background requirement. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q28. Can any nationality apply?
**Not covered:** The summary provides no nationality restriction list or universal acceptance statement. Obtain current nationality, residence and sanctions-related eligibility criteria rather than assuming no restrictions exist.

### Q29. Does paying the contribution guarantee citizenship?
**Summary states:** No. Citizenship is subject to due diligence and approval by the competent authorities. Payment alone is not described as an entitlement to approval. (p. 4)

### Q30. What is the due-diligence process?
**Summary states:** Due diligence is an approval condition and a separate fee is listed for adult applicants. **Not covered:** The full checks, decision criteria, required disclosures and follow-up process are not described. (p. 4)

### Q31. Do I need to prove the source of my investment money?
**Clarification required:** The checklist requests banking, employment/business and asset information, but the summary does not define an explicit source-of-funds standard or accepted funding arrangements. Obtain the applicable requirements; do not treat an incomplete description as a waiver. (p. 8)

### Q32. Can a parent, employer or company sponsor my payment?
**Not covered:** Third-party sponsorship, gift funding, company payments and their documentary conditions are not explained. Confirm permitted arrangements before making a transfer.

### Q33. Is a minimum income or net worth required?
**Not covered:** No threshold beyond the printed contribution is stated. The asset report and bank statements do not themselves establish a numerical net-worth test. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q34. Will previous visa refusals or legal problems affect me?
**Not covered:** Their consequences are not explained. Obtain the full history and supporting evidence for an individual case review before giving an eligibility assurance.

### Q35. Is an interview mandatory?
**Summary states:** An interview may be required in rare cases. The source does not define the triggering circumstances or provide a guarantee that a particular applicant will be exempt. (p. 2)

### Q36. Would an interview be online, and who attends?
**Not covered:** Format, location, participants, interpreter support and accommodations are not described. Do not copy Nauru's virtual-interview rule into this program.

### Q37. Is an interview fee included in due diligence?
**Not covered:** No interview fee or explicit inclusion statement is supplied. Obtain the charging terms if an interview is required. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q38. Must I travel for an oath, biometrics or passport collection?
**Clarification required:** No mandatory visits during application are described, but the full procedure for these formalities is not given. Confirm post-approval arrangements before promising that every stage is remote. (pp. 2–3)

### Q39. Can I submit directly, or must I use an authorized agent?
**Not covered:** The summary promotes Elevay but does not state direct-submission rules or supply proof of agent accreditation. Obtain the current submission requirements and identify the responsible submitting party.

### Q40. What are the official stages, appeal rights and refusal procedures?
**Not covered:** No complete sequence, payment milestones, review mechanism, appeal deadline or resubmission policy is provided. Obtain a current process map and written terms before setting expectations.

## 4. Contributions and fees

### Q41. What is the investment route in this summary?
**Summary states:** A non-refundable donation to the National Development Fund. No property, company-equity, university or bank-deposit alternative is described. (p. 4)

### Q42. What contribution is listed for a single applicant?
**Summary states:** A minimum of $90,000. The repeated reference to this amount on page 4 is the same minimum, not a second contribution. Additional fees apply. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q43. What contribution is listed for a family?
**Summary states:** Family packages for two to four members start **from $95,000**. “From” is a starting price, not a complete guaranteed quote for every family composition. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q44. What does an additional dependent cost?
**Summary states:** The contribution table lists “Additional Dependents – $10,000.” **Clarification required:** It does not define the charging threshold or category-specific exceptions. The exercises assume $10,000 per eligible person beyond a four-person family; confirm that interpretation. (p. 4)

### Q45. Is the donation refundable or income-producing?
**Summary states:** It is explicitly non-refundable. No investment return, interest, equity interest or repayment is described. The broader business-opportunity language is not a return on the donation. (pp. 2–4)

### Q46. What government and processing fees are explicitly in USD?
**Summary states:** USD 250 per person for a certificate of registration; USD 150 per person for national ID; and a USD 5,000 submission fee per application, described as flat regardless of family size. (p. 4)

### Q47. What passport fee is printed?
**Summary states:** $350 per passport. Passport validity, renewal, delivery and replacement charges are not supplied. The exercises treat this as USD in the context of the same fee page; confirm in writing. (p. 4)

### Q48. What due-diligence fee is printed?
**Summary states:** $5,000 per adult applicant. The source does not define “adult” for this fee or specify any minor fee. The exercises use age 18+ as an explicit assumption, not a confirmed fee definition. (p. 4)

### Q49. Do the spouse and adult dependent children pay due diligence too?
**Trainer interpretation requiring confirmation:** “Per adult applicant” is applied in the exercises to each adult, including spouse, eligible adult child and eligible parent/grandparent. It is not treated as a principal-only charge. Confirm the age definition and any exceptions. (p. 4)

### Q50. Does the submission fee increase with every family member?
**Summary states:** No, the printed USD 5,000 submission fee is flat per application regardless of family size. Other per-person charges, adult due diligence and the contribution schedule can still increase the total. (p. 4)

### Q51. Can I pay in euros instead of dollars?
**Summary states:** Contributions/payments are accepted in USD and EUR. **Not covered:** A euro-denominated price list, exchange-rate source, conversion date, bank spread and whether each fee follows the same payment rules. Do not treat $95,000 as €95,000. (pp. 2–3)

### Q52. Is the local currency the euro or US dollar?
**Summary states:** The local currency is the São Tomé and Príncipe dobra, abbreviated STN. Accepted program payment currencies are a separate matter. (pp. 2, 6)

### Q53. What are professional fees and other unlisted costs?
**Summary states:** Professional fees are provided on request. **Trainer guidance:** Obtain a written quote and check for applicable medical, police, certification, translation, authentication, transfer, courier and delivery expenses. These are items to clarify, not invented mandatory charges. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q54. Can the fees change, and when do I have to pay?
**Summary states:** Government and Elevay fees are subject to change and may depend on applicant numbers and complexity. **Not covered:** Payment milestones, escrow, authorized accounts, price-lock terms and installment arrangements. Obtain written payment instructions and a quote-validity period. (p. 4)

### Q55. If I withdraw or am refused, what money comes back?
**Clarification required:** The donation is expressly non-refundable, but the full refusal/withdrawal policy for all payments and the stage at which the donation becomes due are not provided. Obtain terms for each contribution and fee rather than promising either full refund or universal non-refundability. (p. 4)

## 5. Worked cost examples

**Trainer-created arithmetic, not live quotations.** All examples assume:

- Dollar-only contribution, passport and due-diligence figures are USD, consistent with the explicitly USD government fees on the same page; confirm before quoting.
- Every person qualifies and is included in one application.
- The family package starts at $95,000 for two to four total people, including the principal.
- Each eligible additional person beyond four adds $10,000 to the contribution.
- An adult is 18+ for calculation purposes; each adult pays $5,000 due diligence.
- No due-diligence fee is inserted for minors solely because none is printed; this requires confirmation.
- Every person receives one registration certificate, national ID and passport: $250 + $150 + $350 = $750 each.
- Professional fees, bank/transfer charges, any interview charges and all unlisted expenses are excluded.

| Example | Assumed contribution | Submission | Adult due diligence | Registration + ID + passports | Illustrative subtotal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A. Single adult | $90,000 | $5,000 | $5,000 | $750 | **$100,750** |
| B. Couple | $95,000 | $5,000 | $10,000 | $1,500 | **$111,500** |
| C. Couple + children aged 10 and 17 | $95,000 | $5,000 | $10,000 | $3,000 | **$113,000** |
| D. Couple + unmarried dependent children aged 17 and 22 | $95,000 | $5,000 | $15,000 | $3,000 | **$118,000** |
| E. Same family as C + eligible dependent parent aged 60 | $105,000 | $5,000 | $15,000 | $3,750 | **$128,750** |
| F. Same family as E + eligible dependent grandparent aged 80 | $115,000 | $5,000 | $20,000 | $4,500 | **$144,500** |

The word “from” in family pricing remains relevant: these are exercises using the printed starting figure. They do not establish that every depicted family is accepted at that base contribution.

### Q56. How is the single-applicant subtotal calculated?
**Calculation:** $90,000 donation + $5,000 submission + $5,000 adult due diligence + $250 registration + $150 national ID + $350 passport = **$100,750**, before excluded costs and subject to the assumptions above.

### Q57. Why is a four-person family not simply $95,000 total?
**Calculation:** For example C, $95,000 contribution + $5,000 submission + $10,000 for two adults' due diligence + $3,000 for four sets of documents = **$113,000**. The contribution is only one component.

### Q58. Why does replacing a minor with an eligible adult child add $5,000?
**Calculation:** Examples C and D have the same number of people and assumed base contribution. Example D has one additional adult for due diligence, adding $5,000. Eligibility and the actual adult fee definition must be confirmed independently.

### Q59. What does adding an eligible adult dependent beyond four add?
**Calculation:** Under the assumptions, $10,000 extra contribution + $5,000 due diligence + $750 documents = **$15,750**, before any other costs. An eligible minor beyond four would add $10,750 if no minor due-diligence fee applies.

### Q60. What formula should a trainee use for budgeting?
**Trainer calculation:** Illustrative subtotal = confirmed contribution + $5,000 submission + ($5,000 × number of fee-paying adults) + ($750 × number of people). Then add professional and every other applicable quoted cost. Verify eligibility, currencies, the family contribution, adult definition and per-person document treatment first. Do not call this formula a current official tariff.

### Q62. Is the current passport's photo page enough?
**Summary states:** No. All current-passport pages are requested, plus the old passport's bio page and current visa page. The source does not explain missing or unavailable old documents. Obtain specific instructions rather than omitting them silently. (p. 8)

### Q63. Who needs a CV?
**Summary states:** The main applicant, spouse and dependents aged 18 or above. History length, format and explanation of employment gaps are not specified. (p. 8)

### Q64. What banking and employment information is required?
**Summary states:** Twelve months of bank statements and an original bank reference for the main applicant; an original employment letter if employed; certified business-license or company documents if a company owner. No minimum balance or letter template is provided. (p. 8)

### Q65. What must the asset report contain?
**Summary states:** The main applicant must provide an original signed asset report, with a draft to be supplied. The summary does not define asset classes, valuations, liabilities or evidence requirements. Obtain the prescribed form. (p. 8)

### Q66. Do I need three professional reference letters?
**Clarification required:** The wording is “three professional referees,” not three letters. Confirm who qualifies, how long they must have known the applicant, what contact information is needed and whether written references are required. (p. 8)

### Q67. Which police certificates are required?
**Summary states:** Original certificates from country of residence and country of origin for applicants aged 18+. The summary does not define origin, treatment of multiple citizenships, past countries of residence or certificate validity. Do not import another program's ten-year history rule. (p. 8)

### Q68. Does a child under 18 need no background checks?
**Clarification required:** The checklist's police-certificate threshold is 18, but the eligibility page says the main applicant and dependents must have a clean background. A document threshold is not a blanket exemption from screening. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q69. What medical tests are required?
**Summary states:** An original medical certificate no older than three months is requested for all applicants. The actual tests, approved providers, exemptions and medical eligibility criteria are not supplied. (p. 8)

### Q70. How recent should proof of address and photographs be?
**Summary states:** Main-applicant address evidence must be no older than three months. The photo must have been taken within six months. Other document validity periods are not specified. (p. 8)

### Q71. Is a digital photo enough, or do I need prints?
**Clarification required:** The checklist requests one JPEG file per applicant, while the photo instructions mention high-quality paper and identical photos. Obtain a confirmed digital/print specification, number of prints, pixel dimensions and file-size limits before ordering. (p. 8)

### Q72. What photo size and composition are stated?
**Summary states:** Approximately 35 × 45 mm, in color; close front view of head and shoulders with the head taking 70–80% of the picture; eyes open and looking at the camera; no hair over the eyes; neutral expression and closed mouth. Plain white background, clear and sharp, with no alterations. (p. 8)

### Q73. Can I wear glasses or a religious head covering?
**Summary states:** Sunglasses are prohibited; tinted prescription glasses may be worn if the eyes are visible. The source says nothing should cover part of the head but supplies no religious or medical exceptions. Request the applicable standard for the applicant. (p. 8)

### Q74. Are translations, notarization and apostilles required?
**Summary states:** The brochure says documents from Hague Convention member countries require apostilles. **Clarification required:** Accepted certifiers, translation language, non-member authentication and exceptions are not explained. Obtain country-specific requirements, especially given the checklist's wrong-country reference. (p. 8)

### Q75. Is this checklist complete and definitely valid for São Tomé and Príncipe?
**Clarification required:** No such assurance is justified by the source. The wrong-country authority reference and digital/print mismatch need correction. Obtain the current official checklist and additional-document policy rather than treating these 18 entries as an exhaustive final instruction. (p. 8)

## 8. Travel and citizenship benefits

### Q76. How many destinations does the brochure claim I can visit?
**Clarification required:** It claims 70 countries and territories, but the detailed list has 69 entries: 14 Americas, 1 Europe, 6 Oceania, 29 Africa, 12 Asia and 7 Middle East. The list also includes visa-on-arrival and ETA entries, so it is not a verified list of 70 strictly visa-free destinations. (pp. 2–3, 5)

### Q77. Does the summary include South Africa, Singapore and Hong Kong?
**Summary states:** Yes, all three are highlighted and listed. The summary provides no stay length, permitted purpose or current entry conditions; verify those before advising travel. (pp. 3, 5)

### Q78. Does it give me access to Portugal because the country speaks Portuguese?
**Not covered:** Portuguese language and Lusophone connections are described, but no automatic Portuguese residence, employment or citizenship entitlement is supplied. Portugal is not in the destination list. Do not infer migration rights from shared language. (pp. 2–3, 5–6)

### Q79. Does it include the UK, Schengen area, United States or Canada?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not establish access to those destinations. Kosovo is the only destination in its Europe category. Verify actual destination rules rather than transferring benefits from another passport. (p. 5)

### Q80. Can I work, study or settle in a listed destination?
**Not covered:** The list describes travel access categories, not foreign work, study or residence rights. Ask the customer about destination, purpose and duration, then obtain applicable requirements.

### Q81. What do the asterisks mean?
**Summary states:** One asterisk means visa on arrival; two mean ETA. Kenya is the double-asterisk entry. These are the brochure's labels, not independently confirmed current entry classifications. (p. 5)

### Q82. Will my children and grandchildren inherit citizenship automatically?
**Summary states:** Citizenship is described as transferable to future generations. The summary does not give descent conditions, registration requirements, generations, fees or exceptions. Do not promise automatic passports without formalities. (p. 3)

### Q83. How long is the passport valid, and what does renewal cost?
**Not covered:** Validity by age, renewal requirements, replacement costs, passport delivery and renewal pricing are absent. The $350 issuance fee is not a verified renewal fee. (p. 4)

### Q84. Can citizenship be revoked, and are there ongoing obligations?
**Not covered:** Revocation, loss of nationality, reporting, military or civic duties and ongoing status requirements are not comprehensively described. Obtain the relevant law and individual advice before making long-term assurances.

### Q85. Will citizenship lower my taxes or make banking private?
**Clarification required:** The source makes general claims about international tax planning and financial privacy, but gives no tax rates, residence tests, exemptions, reporting obligations or banking-disclosure rules. It cannot establish the customer's tax position or exemption from lawful disclosure. (p. 3)

## 9. Relocating and doing business

### Q86. Where is São Tomé and Príncipe, and what is the capital?
**Summary states:** It is in the Gulf of Guinea, described as Central/West Africa; its capital is São Tomé. The country description mentions two main islands of volcanic origin, beaches and tropical jungle. (p. 6)

### Q87. What language, government and time zone are described?
**Summary states:** Portuguese is official, with Forro, Angolar and Principense listed as minority languages and some French/English. Government is described as a unitary semi-presidential republic, and the time zone as UTC+0. (p. 6)

### Q88. What is the climate like according to the summary?
**Summary states:** It describes a moderate tropical climate and natural landscapes, with a dry season from June to September and wet season from October to May. These are brochure descriptions, not a current weather or location-specific climate assessment. (pp. 3, 6)

### Q89. What business opportunities are mentioned?
**Summary states:** Eco-tourism, agriculture and fisheries, with trade connections in Central Africa and the Portuguese-speaking world. The brochure does not establish a business license, preferential market access, financing or guaranteed returns from citizenship. (pp. 2–3)

### Q90. Can I buy a business or property instead of donating?
**Not covered:** Business opportunities are marketing context, not a stated alternative qualifying route. The only investment criterion described is the Fund contribution. (pp. 2–4)

### Q91. What will housing, schools and healthcare cost my family?
**Not covered:** No rents, school fees, hospital details, insurance costs or household budgets are supplied. The general healthcare and education benefits language does not establish free services or admissions. Build a separate relocation brief for the family's needs. (p. 3)

### Q92. What about jobs, flights, internet, banking, safety and transport?
**Not covered:** Employment, salaries, routes, connectivity, account opening, driving, security and transport services are not detailed. The brochure calls the environment safe and welcoming, but provides no supporting current assessment. Obtain practical information for the proposed location. (p. 6)

### Q93. Are the country statistics and rankings reliable enough for my relocation plan?
**Clarification required:** The summary supplies statistics without observation dates and calls the country “Africa's smallest state” without defining the comparison. Age-structure percentages are printed without age bands. Preserve these as brochure statements, not verified planning facts; Appendix B records the readable data without inventing missing labels. (p. 6)

## 11. Consultation workflow and discovery questions

**Trainer-created workflow, not the official application sequence.**

1. **Define the goal:** Citizenship, travel flexibility, practical relocation, family planning or business. Record the intended destination/activity if the goal concerns another country.
2. **Map the family:** Exact ages, relationships, dependency and marital status. Flag principal age 18, children near 30 and parents/grandparents around 55.
3. **Obtain eligibility rules:** Nationality/residence restrictions, background issues, permitted funding, dependency evidence and approved submission arrangements.
4. **Build the quote:** Confirm total family size, “from” contribution, additional-dependent pricing, adult definition, currencies, flat submission and per-person fees.
5. **Correct the checklist:** Resolve wrong-country references, digital/printed photo format, referee requirements, authentication and additional documents.
6. **Set process expectations:** Written timeline assumptions, possible interview, payment stages, refund terms, approvals and passport delivery.
7. **Prepare a document tracker:** Owner, issuing authority, format, authentication, freshness deadline and completion status for each item.
8. **Plan relocation separately:** Obtain schools, housing, healthcare, work and personal tax information where relevant.

### Suggested discovery prompts

- Why do you want this citizenship, and what must it enable you to do?
- Will you actually move to São Tomé and Príncipe?
- Which destinations, travel purposes and stay lengths matter most?
- Who is applying, and what are their exact ages, relationships and marital status?
- How are adult children and parents/grandparents supported?
- Does anyone have an upcoming birthday, marriage or other eligibility-sensitive event?
- What citizenships, countries of origin and residence histories need assessment?
- What funds will pay the donation and fees, and in what currency?
- What is your complete budget, including professional and document costs?
- Is there a school, family or business deadline driving the desired timeline?

## 13. Role-play exercises

### Scenario A: Single applicant with a $90,000 budget

**Customer:** “I have the advertised amount ready. Is that enough?”

**Expected response:** Explain contribution versus total cost. Calculate the $100,750 illustrative subtotal under stated assumptions, then identify professional and unlisted costs. Do not call the brochure minimum an all-inclusive price.

### Scenario B: Family of four with an adult child

**Customer:** “My spouse, our 17-year-old and our unmarried dependent 22-year-old will join.”

**Expected response:** Confirm dependency and the family package. Under the exercise assumptions, identify three adults and calculate $118,000. Explain that adult eligibility and adult fee definition are distinct matters and both need confirmation.

### Scenario C: Fifth family member is a parent

**Customer:** “My mother is exactly 55. Add her for $10,000.”

**Expected response:** Flag the age wording, relationship and dependency issues. Explain that, if eligible and the pricing assumptions apply, an additional adult adds $15,750 before unlisted costs, not merely $10,000. Obtain the actual determination and quote.

### Scenario D: Paying euros

**Customer:** “I will send €95,000 because the family contribution is $95,000.”

**Expected response:** Explain accepted currency versus quoted denomination. Obtain authorized EUR payment instructions, rate and value date; do not assume dollar-euro parity or invent an exchange rate.

### Scenario E: Portuguese-speaking-country misconception

**Customer:** “This should let me move to Portugal and work there.”

**Expected response:** The brochure mentions language and business connections, not automatic Portuguese residence or employment. Ask about the intended activity and obtain separate destination rules before recommending the program for that objective.

### Scenario F: Customer questions source quality

**Customer:** “The checklist names Vanuatu, and the travel list doesn't match the headline. Why should I proceed?”

**Expected response:** Acknowledge the source defects plainly. Explain the need for corrected official instructions, current eligibility and a destination-specific check. Do not minimize the inconsistencies or assert that correcting the label alone proves every listed requirement valid.
