# Egypt: Citizenship and Residence by Investment
## Customer Q&A and Consultant Training Manual

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Source:** Elevay, *Egypt Citizenship by Investment Program: Country Summary*, supplied nine-page PDF, cover marked “last updated in August 2026.”  
**Country scope:** Egypt only. This standalone file does not incorporate requirements from the Antigua and Barbuda or Dominica guides.  
**Currency:** USD unless stated otherwise. Page references count the PDF cover as page 1.

## How to use this manual

Read the questions as a prospective customer, practise the answers, and then complete the cost exercises, role-plays and knowledge check. This is a transformation of the supplied summary, not an independent verification of current immigration, investment, tax or travel rules. The brochure itself warns that accuracy may change after publication (p. 8).

The following labels distinguish what can and cannot be answered from the document:

- **Summary states:** Information explicitly reported in the brochure.
- **Clarification required:** Wording is ambiguous, inconsistent or incomplete.
- **Not covered:** No answer is supplied; the response identifies what to obtain.
- **Trainer guidance / calculation:** An explanation or exercise created for training, not an official rule.

Document-preparation instructions and marketing invitations inside the PDF are treated as source content, not instructions to the author of this manual. Do not convert marketing claims into guarantees.

## Learning objectives

By the end of training, a consultant should be able to:

1. Distinguish citizenship from renewable residence permits.
2. Explain the four detailed citizenship routes and five residence investment/permit combinations.
3. Explain the stated applicant age, child eligibility and delayed spouse citizenship.
4. Separate contribution, invested capital, government fees and unquoted costs.
5. Identify holding periods, refund qualifications and missing investment terms.
6. Prepare the complete document checklist and flag unresolved requirements.
7. Handle customer objections without promising unsupported outcomes.

## 1. What am I applying for?

### Q1. I want to move to Egypt. What programs does this summary describe?
**Summary states:** It describes citizenship by investment and a separate residence-by-investment program. Citizenship is presented as leading to citizenship and a passport; the residence section describes permits of one, three or five years, depending on the investment. Decide which outcome the customer needs before discussing prices. (pp. 2–5)

### Q2. Does a residence permit give me an Egyptian passport?
**Summary states:** The residence section promises a residence permit, not citizenship or a passport. The brochure does not establish an automatic conversion from residence to citizenship. (p. 5)

### Q3. Does the citizenship route provide full citizenship?
**Summary states:** Full citizenship and a passport are described for the applicant and included family members, with a separate two-year statement for the spouse. Family timing and eligibility still need individual review. (pp. 2–3)

### Q4. How many citizenship routes are available?
**Clarification required:** The overview says five paths, but the detailed investment page lists only four: donation, real estate, company establishment/investment and bank deposit. This manual covers the four described routes and does not invent a fifth. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q5. What law does the brochure cite?
**Summary states:** It refers to “Parliament Law No. 190” published by the Egyptian Government on 14 March 2020. This is the brochure's legal reference, not an independently verified citation or a complete account of amendments. Obtain the applicable legal text for live case advice. (p. 4)

### Q6. How long does citizenship take?
**Summary states:** Six to nine months is the stated guideline. The footnote warns that additional due-diligence requirements can cause longer processing. It does not define when the clock starts. (pp. 2–3)

### Q7. Can you guarantee approval within nine months?
**Summary states:** No guarantee is provided. The summary warns of delays and says the government can reject an application at its sole discretion. A timeline estimate must not be sold as an approval promise. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q8. Do I have to live in Egypt before applying?
**Summary states:** No residency requirement is listed for citizenship. The benefits page says there are no mandatory visits during the application process. Do not automatically apply that statement to the separate residence program or to unaddressed post-approval formalities. (pp. 2–3)

### Q9. Can I keep my current nationality?
**Summary states:** Dual citizenship is allowed. The brochure does not explain the rules of the applicant's existing country, permission procedures or notification duties. Those must be checked separately. (p. 2)

### Q10. Must I speak Arabic, pass an exam or attend an interview?
**Not covered:** No language, examination or interview rules are supplied. Unlike some other summaries, this document does not expressly waive these requirements. Obtain the current rules instead of interpreting silence as an exemption.

## 2. Can I and my family qualify?

### Q11. What is the minimum age for the main applicant?
**Summary states:** The main applicant must be **over 21**. Do not rewrite that as “21 or older”; confirm eligibility for a person exactly 21 and the date at which age is assessed. (p. 4)

### Q12. Can citizens of any country apply?
**Summary states:** Citizens from all countries can apply, but the government retains the right to reject any application at its sole discretion. This is not a guarantee of acceptance for every nationality or person. (p. 4)

### Q13. Do we need a clean criminal record?
**Summary states:** The main applicant and dependents must have a clear background with no criminal record. The document checklist separately gives police-certificate requirements for applicants aged 16 and above. (pp. 4, 8)

### Q14. Can my children be included?
**Summary states:** Dependent children under 21 may be included. The brochure does not define dependency, student status, marital status or the relevant age-assessment date. (p. 2)

### Q15. Can my child who is exactly 21 join as a dependent?
**Clarification required:** “Under 21” does not include age 21 on its face. No exception or independent-applicant solution for that exact age is supplied, and the main-applicant rule says over 21. Obtain a written eligibility assessment. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q16. What if my child turns 21 while the case is processing?
**Not covered:** The summary does not explain whether age is measured at submission, approval or another milestone, or whether eligibility is protected during processing. Resolve this before submitting an age-sensitive case.

### Q17. Will my spouse receive citizenship at the same time as I do?
**Summary states:** The summary says the spouse obtains citizenship after two years. It does not define the event that starts that period, application steps, continued-marriage conditions or whether different rules apply depending on the principal applicant's sex. Do not promise simultaneous citizenship. (pp. 2–3)

### Q18. Does the two-year period start when I pay or submit the application?
**Not covered:** The starting event is not stated. Do not calculate a spouse's promised citizenship date from the investment or submission date without confirmation. (pp. 2–3)

### Q19. Can my spouse live and work in Egypt while waiting?
**Not covered:** Interim residence, work permission and family sponsorship during the two-year period are not explained. Arrange a separate status assessment for the spouse.

### Q20. Can I include parents or grandparents?
**Clarification required:** The family overview names children and spouse, not parents or grandparents. The document checklist nevertheless mentions dependent parents in the marriage/divorce certificate entry. That reference does not establish parent eligibility; request a written rule. (pp. 2, 8)

### Q21. Can I include siblings, stepchildren or adopted children?
**Not covered:** The summary does not define these categories. For stepchildren and adopted children, obtain relationship, custody and consent requirements; for siblings, do not promise eligibility from another country's rules.

### Q22. Is there an exception for a disabled adult child?
**Not covered:** No disability-related dependent exception is supplied. Obtain an individual determination rather than importing an exception from a previous guide.

### Q23. Can I add a spouse or newborn after approval?
**Not covered:** Later additions, birth registration, fees and deadlines are not described. The claim about future generations is not a complete later-addition procedure. (p. 3)

### Q24. Can future generations receive citizenship?
**Summary states:** Citizenship is described as transferable to future generations. The summary does not specify descent rules, registration, generations, parentage conditions or documentation. Do not promise automatic passports without formalities. (p. 3)

### Q25. Do I need a minimum income, net worth or business background?
**Not covered:** No separate financial or experience thresholds are stated beyond the route investments. Required source-of-funds evidence and whether third-party funding is accepted are also not defined.

## 3. Citizenship investment routes

### Q26. What are the headline investment amounts?
**Summary states:** The detailed page gives the following figures. Each is separate from the $10,000 government fee and unquoted professional charges. (p. 4)

| Citizenship route | Stated requirement | Holding/refund information in the brochure |
|---|---:|---|
| Donation | $250,000 contribution | Explicitly non-refundable |
| Real estate | At least $300,000 in one or more properties | Hold for at least 5 years |
| Company | At least $350,000 investment + $100,000 donation | No holding period or investment-exit terms supplied |
| Bank deposit | $500,000 | Refunded after 3 years without interest, at the local exchange rate at refund |

### Q27. How does the donation route work?
**Summary states:** The investor makes a non-refundable $250,000 contribution to the Citizenship by Investment Unit. The summary does not give payment milestones, authorized account details or conditions for paying before approval. (p. 4)

### Q28. Will the donation be returned if I change my mind?
**Summary states:** It is expressly non-refundable. The brochure does not explain the full refusal/withdrawal treatment or the timing of every payment. Obtain written terms before transferring money. (p. 4)

### Q29. Does the $250,000 cover a whole family?
**Clarification required:** No family-size contribution schedule is supplied. Do not assume either a separate $250,000 per person or an unlimited family package. Confirm the contribution and all fees for the proposed family. (pp. 2, 4)

### Q30. Can I buy multiple properties instead of one?
**Summary states:** Yes, the citizenship route permits one or more properties with a minimum combined investment of $300,000. Valuation methods, ownership proportions and eligible property types are not defined. (p. 4)

### Q31. Can I buy any property, including one I already own?
**Not covered:** The summary does not explain eligible sellers, approved locations, existing ownership, purchase dates, off-plan property, registration standards or foreign-ownership restrictions. Confirm eligibility before committing to a property.

### Q32. How long must I hold the citizenship property?
**Summary states:** At least five years. The summary does not state whether the period starts at purchase, registration, investment completion or citizenship approval. Obtain the relevant start date in writing. (p. 4)

### Q33. Can I sell early, replace the property or use it as loan collateral?
**Not covered:** Early disposal, substitution, mortgage and compliance consequences are not explained. Do not assume any of these actions preserve eligibility.

### Q34. Can I live in the property or rent it out?
**Not covered:** Occupancy, rental rights, management, permits, tax, insurance and ongoing ownership costs are not detailed. Obtain property-specific terms and a separate cost estimate.

### Q35. Is property appreciation or resale guaranteed?
**Not covered:** No return, resale price, buyer or buyback is guaranteed. The brochure's economic forecasts do not establish an investment return. (pp. 3–4)

### Q36. Must I start a new company?
**Summary states:** No. The company route describes establishing a new company in Egypt or investing in a previously established company. (p. 4)

### Q37. Is the company route $350,000 or $450,000?
**Summary states:** Both components are required: at least $350,000 invested in the company **plus** a $100,000 donation to the Government of Egypt. That totals $450,000 before government and professional fees. (p. 4)

### Q38. Can I recover all $450,000 by selling my company shares?
**Clarification required:** The $100,000 is labeled a donation, not equity. The $350,000 investment's exit terms, value and liquidity are not supplied. Do not present the entire sum as recoverable invested capital. (p. 4)

### Q39. What shareholding, sectors, jobs or management duties are required?
**Not covered:** Minimum ownership percentage, permitted activities, employment creation, operating duties and company holding period are not provided. Obtain current route-specific conditions before recommending a business.

### Q40. How does the citizenship bank deposit work?
**Summary states:** A $500,000 deposit is described, refundable after three years without interest at the local exchange rate at the time of refund. The precise refund currency, formula, bank and release procedure are not fully stated. (p. 4)

### Q41. Will I receive $500,000 back in US dollars?
**Clarification required:** The brochure does not promise repayment in USD; it refers to the local exchange rate at refund. Confirm the repayment currency, conversion basis, bank charges and ability to reconvert or transfer funds. Do not promise a fixed USD recovery. (p. 4)

### Q42. Will the citizenship deposit earn interest?
**Summary states:** No interest is described: the deposit is said to be refunded without interest. Fees and the value of having the money unavailable should be considered separately from the headline deposit. (p. 4)

### Q43. Can I withdraw the deposit early or use a loan to fund it?
**Not covered:** Early withdrawal, financing, collateral, source-country transfers and accepted payment channels are not explained. Obtain the deposit agreement and program conditions.

### Q44. Which route is cheapest or best for me?
**Trainer guidance:** The donation has the smallest stated citizenship contribution, but is non-refundable. The property, company and deposit routes have different cash requirements and potential recovery conditions. Compare the complete costs, liquidity needs, risks and family rules before recommending a route; the brochure alone cannot determine the best individual choice.

### Q45. Can I combine a smaller donation and smaller property purchase to qualify?
**Not covered:** No combination route or partial-credit mechanism is described. The company route's specified investment-plus-donation structure is not evidence that arbitrary combinations are accepted.

## 4. Fees, payments and worked citizenship budgets

### Q46. What government fee is listed?
**Summary states:** $10,000 in addition to the required investment. The brochure does not specify whether it is per person, per application or subject to additional family charges. Confirm the charging basis. (p. 4)

### Q47. What do professional services cost?
**Summary states:** Professional fees are available upon request. Obtain a written amount, scope, exclusions, payment stages and refund terms. (p. 4)

### Q48. Are due diligence, passports, interviews and certificates free?
**Clarification required:** No separate prices are given for these items. Missing fee lines do not establish that they are free or included in the $10,000. Request a full itemized quotation. (p. 4)

### Q49. What other expenses might I need to budget for?
**Trainer guidance:** Ask about applicable medical, police-certificate, translation, authentication, courier, banking, legal, property-registration and company-formation costs. These are not priced in the brochure. Identify possible items without inventing amounts or asserting that every item applies.

### Q50. When do I pay, and to whom?
**Not covered:** The summary supplies no payment schedule, escrow arrangements or verified beneficiary account instructions. Obtain authorized written instructions and the corresponding contractual conditions before any transfer.

### Q51. Can I pay in Egyptian pounds or in installments?
**Not covered:** Citizenship investment amounts are quoted in USD. Alternative currencies, installments, transfer origin and exchange-rate calculation are not specified. The residence deposit's allowance for other foreign currencies should not be copied into citizenship payment rules. (pp. 4–5)

### Q52. What are the basic citizenship budget illustrations?
**Trainer calculation:** The following add **one assumed $10,000 government fee** to each minimum route requirement. They are arithmetic exercises, not final quotations. They exclude professional fees, any additional family charges and all unlisted expenses. The government-fee unit must be verified. (Source amounts: p. 4)

| Example | Calculation | Illustrated initial subtotal |
|---|---|---:|
| A. Donation | $250,000 + $10,000 | **$260,000** |
| B. Minimum property | $300,000 + $10,000 | **$310,000** |
| C. Company | $350,000 investment + $100,000 donation + $10,000 | **$460,000** |
| D. Citizenship deposit | $500,000 + $10,000 | **$510,000** |
| E. Two qualifying properties | $180,000 + $120,000 + $10,000 | **$310,000** |
| F. Property above minimum | $340,000 + $10,000 | **$350,000** |

Examples E and F assume the properties and their assessed values qualify. Asset purchases and deposits are not the same as non-refundable fees. Do not subtract hypothetical future resale/refund proceeds to advertise a guaranteed “net cost.”

### Q53. Is the company option only $360,000 after the government fee?
**Calculation:** No. That would omit the $100,000 donation. Using the exercise assumption, $350,000 + $100,000 + $10,000 = **$460,000**, before unquoted costs.

### Q54. Why is the refundable deposit illustration still $510,000 upfront?
**Calculation:** The initial cash requirement includes the full $500,000 deposit and the assumed $10,000 fee. The brochure describes a later refund of the deposit under its terms, not an exemption from funding it initially or a refund of the government fee. (p. 4)

### Q55. Can you calculate an all-inclusive price for my spouse and children?
**Clarification required:** Not from this summary alone. It lacks a family fee schedule, professional price and several charging details. Collect the exact family composition, confirm eligibility and spouse timing, then request a complete written quote.

## 5. Residence by investment: a separate outcome

### Q56. What is the lowest investment described for residence?
**Summary states:** $50,000 for a one-year residence permit through either qualifying property ownership or a bank deposit. This is a residence threshold, not a citizenship or passport price. Fees are not provided. (p. 5)

### Q57. What property values correspond to the different permit lengths?
**Summary states:** One or more properties in Egypt with these total values are described as qualifying for the following permits, with the possibility of renewal. (p. 5)

| Total qualifying property value | Residence permit described |
|---:|---|
| At least $50,000 | 1 year |
| At least $100,000 | 3 years |
| At least $200,000 | 5 years |

### Q58. What residence bank deposits are described?
**Summary states:** A $50,000 deposit, or its equivalent in other foreign currencies, for a one-year permit; a $100,000 deposit, or its equivalent in other foreign currencies, for a three-year permit. A five-year bank-deposit residence option is not supplied. (p. 5)

### Q59. Can two properties be counted toward residence?
**Summary states:** Yes. The residence property section refers to ownership of one or more properties with the specified total value. The valuation, title and ownership-share rules are not detailed. (p. 5)

### Q60. Does buying $200,000 of property qualify me for citizenship?
**Summary states:** That amount appears as a five-year **residence** property threshold. The detailed **citizenship** property minimum is $300,000. Do not merge the two schedules. (pp. 4–5)

### Q61. Are residence permits renewable automatically?
**Clarification required:** The property-based permits are described as having the possibility of renewal. That is not an unconditional renewal guarantee. Renewal fees, continued-investment conditions and application deadlines are not supplied. Bank-deposit permit renewal terms are not expressly stated. (p. 5)

### Q62. Does a five-year permit mean permanent residence?
**Summary states:** The brochure describes a five-year permit, with possible renewal, not permanent residence. No permanent-status entitlement is set out. (p. 5)

### Q63. Can I eventually exchange residence for citizenship without another investment?
**Not covered:** No automatic progression, residence-credit rule or investment-offset arrangement is described. Obtain the applicable naturalization or program rules rather than promising a conversion.

### Q64. Can my family join a residence application?
**Not covered:** The residence section does not define spouse or dependent eligibility. Citizenship child and spouse provisions must not be assumed to govern residence permits. (pp. 2, 5)

### Q65. Does the residence permit let me work or operate a business?
**Not covered:** Employment, self-employment, licensing and business-operation rights are not described. Confirm the proposed activity and any required authorizations.

### Q66. What residence documents and fees are required?
**Summary states:** The Director of Passport, Immigration, and Nationality Administration determines residence requirements and documents. The brochure supplies no complete residence checklist or fee schedule and directs readers to a consultant for further details. Do not automatically apply the citizenship fee or Appendix A unchanged. (p. 5)

### Q67. How long must the residence deposit remain in the bank?
**Not covered:** Lock-in, interest, refund currency, renewal and early withdrawal terms are not stated. Do not copy the citizenship deposit's three-year, no-interest terms into the residence route. (pp. 4–5)

### Q68. Can I sell the residence property or spend most of the year abroad?
**Not covered:** Continued ownership, permitted absences, minimum stay and consequences of sale are not explained. Obtain permit-specific conditions before planning a sale or prolonged absence.

### Q69. Does $100,000 give me the same outcome through property and deposit?
**Summary states:** Both are described as routes to a three-year residence permit. Their ownership, liquidity, renewal and operating terms are not compared in the brochure, so they should not be presented as financially identical. (p. 5)

### Q70. Can you give examples of residence eligibility calculations?
**Trainer calculations:** These illustrate thresholds only; all assets must qualify and all unquoted fees remain additional.

| Example | Calculation | Brochure outcome illustrated |
|---|---|---|
| G. One property | $50,000 | 1-year property-based residence |
| H. Two properties | $60,000 + $40,000 = $100,000 | 3-year property-based residence |
| I. Two properties | $120,000 + $80,000 = $200,000 | 5-year property-based residence |
| J. Residence bank deposit | $100,000 | 3-year deposit-based residence |

None of these calculations promises a passport, automatic renewal or a complete application price. (p. 5)

| 6 | Twelve passport-sized photographs, taken within 6 months | All applicants |

### Q72. Do you need my actual passport or just copies?
**Summary states:** Both certified copies and the original current passport are listed. Obtain clear instructions on whether the original is inspected or retained, for how long, and how it is securely returned. The summary does not explain those logistics. (p. 8)

### Q73. Which countries must issue police certificates?
**Summary states:** For applicants aged 16+, certificates are requested for every country lived in for more than one year during the past ten years, plus the country of citizenship. The summary does not explain cumulative versus continuous residence, multiple citizenships or certificate validity periods. (p. 8)

### Q74. I have never lived in my citizenship country. Is its certificate still needed?
**Summary states:** The checklist mentions a waiver if the applicant never lived there and can provide full substantiating information. **Clarification required:** The authority granting the waiver and acceptable proof are not identified. Do not promise that an adviser can waive an official requirement independently. (p. 8)

### Q75. Do I need an Egyptian police certificate even if I have never visited Egypt?
**Clarification required:** An original Egyptian police certificate is separately listed without conditions. Obtain applicability and issuance instructions for a non-resident. This also needs coordination with the claim that no mandatory visits are needed during application. (pp. 3, 8)

### Q76. What medical tests are needed, and do children need them?
**Summary states:** An original medical test result no older than three months is required for all applicants. Specific tests, approved clinics, age exceptions and the effect of findings on eligibility are not supplied. Do not import another country's HIV or blood-test rules. (p. 8)

### Q77. What does “Endorsement of the Nationalities” mean?
**Not covered:** The checklist uses this label without defining the document, issuing authority, format or process. Ask the case team for an exact specimen and instructions rather than guessing that it means a passport copy or citizenship declaration. (p. 8)

### Q78. What investment proof should I provide?
**Clarification required:** The checklist asks for documentation showing the required investment, but gives no accepted evidence list. Obtain route-specific instructions for donation receipts, property title/payment evidence, company investment records or bank deposit confirmation. These are topics to clarify, not a confirmed substitute checklist. (p. 8)

### Q79. Do my documents need apostilles or translations?
**Summary states:** The brochure says documents issued in Hague Convention member countries require an apostille. **Clarification required:** It does not establish the receiving authority's accepted authentication chain, translations, consular legalization or non-member-country process. Obtain country-specific instructions before paying for authentication. (p. 8)

### Q80. How many photos do we need, and what size?
**Summary states:** Twelve per applicant, approximately 35 × 45 mm, in color on high-quality paper, taken within the past six months. (p. 8)

### Q81. What should the photos look like?
**Summary states:** Front-facing close view of head and shoulders; the head occupies 70–80% of the photograph; eyes open and looking at the camera; no hair over the eyes; neutral expression with closed mouth; plain white background; clear, sharp, unaltered image. All photos should be identical. (p. 8)

### Q82. Can I wear glasses or a head covering?
**Summary states:** No sunglasses; tinted prescription glasses may be worn if eyes remain visible. The source says nothing should cover part of the head but does not explain religious or medical exceptions. Obtain the applicable photo standard for the applicant. (p. 8)

### Q83. What are the application stages, payment milestones and appeal rights?
**Not covered:** The brochure does not supply a full sequence, submission-agent rules, appeal process or refusal-review deadlines. Obtain a current process map before setting expectations or scheduling payments.

## 7. Passport benefits, taxes and obligations

### Q84. How many destinations can I visit with the passport?
**Clarification required:** Page 2 says 66 countries; pages 3 and 6 say 50 countries and territories. Page 6 also marks many entries as visa on arrival. The source cannot support a single verified count of strictly visa-free destinations. Its full list is preserved in Appendix A. (pp. 2–3, 6)

### Q85. Does it provide visa-free entry to the UK, EU, US or Canada?
**Not covered:** The supplied list does not establish such broad access. Verify the actual destination, purpose, length of stay and authorization requirements before advising travel. A headline count is not a substitute for destination rules. (p. 6)

### Q86. Can I work, study or settle in a listed destination?
**Not covered:** The travel list gives no work, study or residence rights abroad. Visa-free or visa-on-arrival claims should not be translated into permission to migrate to another country.

### Q87. Will Egyptian citizenship reduce or eliminate my taxes?
**Clarification required:** The brochure makes a broad statement about dual citizenship as a tool for international tax planning but supplies no Egyptian tax rates, residence tests, exemptions or analysis of obligations elsewhere. It does not support a personal tax-saving promise. (p. 3)

### Q88. Does the passport make my banking or investments confidential?
**Clarification required:** The source mentions financial privacy but gives no banking-disclosure, beneficial-ownership or reporting rules. Do not present citizenship as exemption from lawful reporting or transparency obligations. Obtain specific advice for the intended financial arrangement. (p. 3)

### Q89. Are there military service, voting, renewal or other citizenship obligations?
**Not covered:** Military service, civic duties, passport validity, renewal costs, revocation grounds and loss-of-nationality rules are not explained. These are important follow-up topics for an individual family, especially before planning a move.

### Q90. Will we automatically get free healthcare and education?
**Clarification required:** The brochure promotes access to healthcare and education opportunities but provides no entitlement, enrollment, fee or insurance rules. Do not promise free services or automatic school/hospital admission. (p. 3)

## 8. Living in Egypt and choosing an adviser

### Q91. What basic country information is given?
**Summary states:** Egypt is described as in Northeast Africa, with Cairo as capital, Arabic as its language, the Egyptian pound as currency and a presidential republic as its government type. The brochure lists an area of 1,010,408 km². (p. 6)

**Summary states:** It traces Egypt's Nile Delta heritage to the sixth–fourth millennia BCE and highlights writing, agriculture, urbanization, religion and central government. **Not covered:** Job availability, salaries, account opening, mortgages, insurance, local safety, driving, public transport and connectivity are not detailed. Record the client's priorities and research them separately before recommending a relocation plan.

## 9. Consultation workflow

**Trainer-created workflow, not an official application sequence.**

1. **Establish the desired status.** Does the client want a residence permit, citizenship, a passport or practical relocation support?
2. **Map the family.** Record relationships, birth dates, dependency, citizenships, residence history and the spouse's planned status while waiting.
3. **Identify eligibility issues.** Flag exact age 21, children approaching 21, parents, criminal history, unclear documents and source-of-funds questions.
4. **Compare appropriate routes.** Explain contribution versus property/company investment versus deposit; separate residence thresholds from citizenship amounts.
5. **Calculate transparent cash needs.** Itemize the investment, donation where relevant, confirmed government fees, professional fees and remaining unknowns.
6. **Explain liquidity and timing.** Address the five-year property hold, three-year citizenship deposit, unspecified company exit and non-refundable contribution.
7. **Prepare documents.** Assign ownership, issuing country, authentication instructions and expiry checks to every item.
8. **Obtain written clarifications.** Resolve family rules, fee units, spouse timeline, refund mechanics and process before a binding quote or payment.
9. **Plan relocation separately.** Housing, schools, work, healthcare and personal taxes require information beyond the brochure.

### Discovery questions

- Do you primarily want to live in Egypt or obtain Egyptian citizenship?
- Who should be included, and what are their exact ages and relationships?
- Is anyone close to the age-21 threshold?
- Can the spouse's plans accommodate a different citizenship timeline?
- What funds are available initially, and when might you need them back?
- Do you prefer a contribution, property, business involvement or bank deposit?
- Do you already own Egyptian property or a company interest?
- What citizenships and residence histories must be documented?
- Is there a fixed school, business or family deadline?
- What city, housing, healthcare, school and employment needs would relocation involve?

## 11. Role-play exercises

### Scenario A: Residence mistaken for citizenship

**Customer:** “I can buy a $50,000 apartment. When will my Egyptian passport arrive?”

**Expected response:** Explain that the amount is associated with one-year residence, not citizenship. Ask whether a residence permit satisfies the goal. Present citizenship amounts separately, and do not promise automatic conversion.

### Scenario B: Family wants simultaneous passports

**Customer:** “My spouse, my 20-year-old child and I need passports before next summer.”

**Expected response:** Explain the six-to-nine-month guideline and possible delays, the spouse's separate two-year statement, and the child's approaching age threshold. Seek the age-assessment and spouse-period rules before accepting the proposed timeline.

### Scenario C: Company investor misses the donation

**Customer:** “I have $360,000, so I can invest $350,000 and pay the fee.”

**Expected response:** Explain the additional $100,000 donation. The illustrative minimum is $460,000 with one assumed government fee, before professional and unlisted expenses. Do not treat the donation as business equity.

### Scenario D: Deposit applicant wants dollar certainty

**Customer:** “I will recover exactly $500,000 in dollars after three years, correct?”

**Expected response:** Quote the source's substantive terms: refund after three years, no interest, local exchange rate at refund. Explain that payout currency and mechanics need written confirmation. Do not promise a fixed USD recovery or invent a conversion formula.

### Scenario E: Property investor needs liquidity

**Customer:** “I will buy two properties for $180,000 and $120,000, then sell one next year.”

**Expected response:** The combined value reaches the stated citizenship threshold if both qualify, but the summary requires a five-year hold. Explain that partial sale, substitution and consequences are not defined. Do not approve the proposed sale plan.

### Scenario F: Documentation and family ambiguity

**Customer:** “Your checklist mentions dependent parents, so include my mother. Also, I have never lived in my citizenship country, so skip that police certificate.”

**Expected response:** A document entry does not establish parent eligibility. The possible certificate waiver requires evidence and a competent decision; do not omit it without confirmation. Obtain the missing eligibility and waiver instructions.
